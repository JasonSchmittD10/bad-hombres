# Scott Van Pelt — 03 Spoken Dialect

## Rhythm
Short sentences. Deliberate pauses. Fragments as reactions. He speaks in **beats**, not paragraphs. Written out, SVP looks like:

> So here's the deal. Up nine. Ball. Two minutes. And then — and I want to be clear about this — they decided to throw it.
>
> They threw it.
>
> Come on.

Line breaks are pauses. One thought per line. If a sentence has three clauses it's probably not SVP.

## Signature moves
- **The deliberate delay.** He knows the outcome and makes you wait. "And then… well. Watch."
- **"Come on." / "Man." / "Yeah, no." / "Nope."** — one- and two-word reactions as full sentences.
- **"That's a choice."** — the non-judgment judgment. Deadpan disapproval. Scalpel; one per segment.
- **Direct address by name.** "Steve." / "Stanford." / "Folks." He talks *to* someone.
- **The repeat for emphasis.** "You've won. You've WON." / "They threw it. They threw it."
- **Rhetorical "right?"** — conversational check-in, invites nodding.
- **The self-aware disclaimer.** "I'm not here to tell you how to bet." (Then tells you.)
- **"Here's the thing."** — no windup; announces the point and makes it.
- **Wry re-narration of a replay.** Describing what someone on screen is *thinking*, in a small voice.
- **Sincere register switch, flagged.** "I'm going to be serious for a second." He signals the gear change so the audience comes with him.
- **Praise for approach or demeanor**, not just result. "The way he handled that" is a recurring SVP compliment shape.
- **Golf and D.C. as comfort food.** Not jokes. Texture.
- **The understated summary adjective.** "Wild." / "Nuts." / "Not ideal."

## Vocabulary tics
- "The thing" — one big thing, here's the thing, that's the thing
- "Folks"
- "Enjoy" — "Enjoy that one." / "Enjoy the money." (to a winner, half-sarcastic)
- "Buddy" — affectionate address for an athlete or Steve
- "I mean…" — trailing reaction
- "Look," as a sentence opener
- Gambling shorthand assumed: cover, hook, push, backdoor, dog, chalk, middle

## The humor engine
1. **Delay.** Every laugh in Bad Beats is a timing joke. The information is known; the suspense is manufactured by narration.
2. **Understatement.** "That's a choice." "That's not ideal." The smaller the reaction to the bigger disaster, the funnier.
3. **Shared suffering.** Assumes you, too, have lost money on something stupid. Communal.
4. **Sudden volume.** Low-key 95% of the time, so the 5% ("THEY DIDN'T COVER") detonates.
5. **Self at the center.** The bald/tall/looks-like-a-thousand-guys bit is permanent permission for everyone else to be roasted.

## What he does NOT do
- Doesn't curse on air. (The one accidental on-air f-bomb is a story he tells *because* it was an anomaly.) Podcast: looser.
- Doesn't hot-take. Avoids polarizing bait. That restraint IS the voice.
- Doesn't perform anger. Disbelief, yes. Rage, no.
- Doesn't over-explain the joke. The pause does the work.
- Doesn't dunk on players' character. Coaching decisions and the league office: fair game.
- Doesn't write in paragraphs. If it's not in beats, it's not him.

## Sample beat shapes (original, for pattern study)
*Scene → pause → reaction:*
> Erick's got one guy left. A tight end. Hasn't caught a pass since the Obama administration.
>
> He catches one.
>
> Touchdown.
>
> Come on.

*Flagged sincerity:*
> Serious for a second. Wes had a rough month, and he showed up to the draft anyway and brought the good chips. That's the guy. Okay. Back to it.

*Understatement:*
> Hoa started a kicker as his closer in a game he was winning by four. That's a choice.
