# Scott Van Pelt — 05 Prompt Kit

## The rules (how to fake it)
1. **Write in beats.** One thought per line. Short. Fragments allowed. Line breaks are pauses.
2. **Open with the best thing you saw.** One item. Sincere.
3. **Set the scene before the score.** Then pause. Then the number. Then one word.
4. **Bad Beats:** line → comfortable position → slow unraveling → dagger → reaction → mock-moral. Find the *obscure* beat (lost by 0.3 on a long-snapper fumble recovery), not the obvious one (lost by 40).
5. **One Big Thing:** state plainly, turn once, make the point once, button. Under 250 words.
6. **One "that's a choice."** Max. It's a scalpel.
7. **One repeat-for-emphasis.** "You've won. You've WON."
8. **One self-deprecating aside, early,** to license the rest.
9. **One flagged sincere moment** if the week earns it. "Serious for a second." Then back.
10. **Sign off warm and short.** No summary.
11. **No profanity, no rage.** Disbelief and volume instead.

## Paste-ready prompt
```
Write this as Scott Van Pelt — the midnight SportsCenter register. This is SPOKEN, not written: one thought per line, short sentences, fragments allowed, line breaks are pauses. No paragraphs longer than three lines.

Sections:
1. THE BEST THING I SAW THIS WEEK — one sincere item, 3–5 lines.
2. BAD BEATS, with [member as Stanford Steve] — for the closest/cruelest loss. Structure: the line → the comfortable position ("You've won. [Name], you've WON.") → the unraveling, one beat per line, slowly → the dagger → the margin → reaction (one word, or silence, or a single CAPS line) → mock-moral ("Never bet on X. Ever."). Address [member] by name mid-beat.
3. ONE BIG THING — under 250 words. "Here's the thing tonight." State it. Setup in 1–2 sentences. "But here's what I keep coming back to." The point, once. Button. "That's it. That's the thing."
4. Sign-off: "Enjoy the games, fellas."

Tone: wry, low-key, warm. Exactly one "That's a choice." One self-deprecating aside early (bald/tall/tired). If the week has a real-life moment, flag it — "Serious for a second." — say it in two clean lines, then "Okay. Back to it." No profanity. No anger — disbelief instead. Don't explain the joke; the pause is the joke.

Data: [scores, closest margins, projections, group chat]
```

## QA checklist
- [ ] No paragraph longer than three lines
- [ ] Opens on something good, sincerely
- [ ] Bad Beats delays the dagger — at least four beats between "you've won" and the margin
- [ ] Someone is addressed by name mid-beat
- [ ] Exactly one "That's a choice."
- [ ] One repeat-for-emphasis
- [ ] One Big Thing is under 250 words and has one point
- [ ] One Big Thing ends "That's it. That's the thing."
- [ ] Sincere moment (if any) is flagged and short
- [ ] No profanity, no rage
- [ ] Sign-off is short and warm
- [ ] Nothing recycled

## Reference material (self-study, not reproduction)
- **"Bad Beats" segments** — ESPN posts them weekly in-season (YouTube). Watch three in a row; structure is identical, variation is entirely pacing. The Boise State–San Jose State edition (Nov 2024) is the "THEY DIDN'T COVER" reference point.
- **"One Big Thing" clips** — ESPN.com video. Pick a serious one (Stuart Scott's daughters, 2024) and a silly one (Red Panda's stolen unicycle, 2025) to see the same structure at both ends of the register.
- **SVPod** — the looser, longer version; the two-person cadence with Stanford Steve is the model for a league duo segment.
- **New Heights, Ep. 132 (2025)** — SVP as a guest, telling stories in his own rhythm without the show's constraints.
- **SI interview, Oct 2015** — his own description of what each segment was built to do (Bad Beats: "perverse pride in finding the more obscure examples"; One Big Thing: radio staple turned "tent pole").
