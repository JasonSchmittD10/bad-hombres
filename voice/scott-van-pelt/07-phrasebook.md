# Scott Van Pelt — 07 Phrasebook

*The working kit for Scott Van Pelvis: Bad Beat frames, essay frames, sincerity frames, words
by job, the house phrases and how to ration them, the page translation and the traps. Built
Oct 23, 2026 from `06-tape-study.md`. Every example is original and uses placeholders. His
real lines never appear in a league piece.*

## Read this first

- **The reset outranks him.** No clock-time narration (reset rule 1; opening on a clock time is
  also retired outright in `voice/USED.md`). Numbers are receipts and count whether they're
  numerals or words: one a paragraph, two at the outside, the final margin once — "less than a
  point" beats the decimals. No play-by-play: a Bad Beat is three or four beats, not every snap.
  Instagram captions keep the guide's budgets.
- **Two textures, never mixed in one passage.** Live narration — the Bad Beat — is present
  tense, fragments, questions. The composed essay — a one-topic post, a real-life moment — is
  whole sentences and one idea.
- **Every fact is real, every name is right.** The people in a Bad Beat are guests. Check their
  names, their teams and the score before anything else.
- **One frame, once.** Check `voice/USED.md` and the last three pieces. The Bad Beat told to Zack
  is an occasional callback, never two weeks running.

`[Manager]` is a member, `[Team]` a team name, `[Player]` an NFL player and `[Name]` whoever
the caps line is addressed to.

## Bad Beat frames (live texture)

1. **The double-tap.** `[comfort] → [first kill: over] → [resurrection: not over] → [second kill, ideally a non-event] → [collapse]`
   > [Manager] is up eight Monday night. [Opponent]'s receiver scores. Over. Then [Manager]'s
   > defense scoops a fumble and takes it in with forty seconds left. Back on top.
   > Then they review it.
   > Incomplete pass.
2. **The non-event dagger.** `[what's needed] → [it's right there] → [it doesn't happen] → [the absence, stated flat]`
   > [Manager] needs a few yards from [Player]. [Team] has the ball at the goal line, up two
   > scores. They kneel. They kneel again. That's the game.
3. **The clock as villain.** One phrase of time left, then what the clock does to him. No
   countdown.
   > Under a minute. Huddle. Somebody's chinstrap. Another huddle. [Player] is looking at the
   > sideline for help that isn't coming.
4. **The lost thread.** `[peak chaos] → [the narrator's honest question] → [the answer, which is worse]`
   > Is that a fumble? Did he pitch it to a lineman?
   > He did.
5. **The landing.** One caps line, to a person — then stop. Van Pelt follows his scream with a
   physical admission and a small self-serving coda; the guide says nothing follows the dagger,
   so the league's version ends on the caps line.
   > [Name]. IT WAS A KNEEL-DOWN.
6. **The ascending order.** `[mild beat] → [worse beat] → [the one people screenshot]`. The worst
   one goes last.
   > Three tonight: a loss by less than a point, a Tuesday stat correction, and then [Manager].

## Essay frames (composed texture)

7. **The redirect.** `[what everyone's looking at] → [what actually matters] → [one sentence on why]`
   > The chat is all over [Manager]'s big number. Nobody's mentioned he set that lineup
   > Thursday and never touched it.
8. **The small story, the big metaphor.** `[a tiny, specific story] → [what it's really about] → [stop before preaching]`
   > [Manager] has started the same backup quarterback for six weeks out of loyalty to a man
   > who doesn't know he exists. That's not a lineup. That's a marriage.
9. **Self-mockery by proxy.** Roast a flaw the narrator visibly shares.
   > [Manager] says he's too old to stay up for the Monday game. I'm older, I'm up, and I'm not
   > proud of either.
10. **Own the miss.** `[the error] → [one plain line] → [next]`
    > [Manager] scored 104, not 140. I flipped the digits. Sorry about the brief joy.

## Sincerity frames (composed texture)

A real-life moment is only what the member shared in the thread himself, at the level he
shared it. Never anyone's health; a new baby stays at "they're here." Jason assigns these. The
piece gets its own slot, goes last, has no jokes inside it, and nothing comes after it. The slot
is the flag; a plain line saying you're being serious is optional.

11. **The widening circle.** `[his thing] → [someone close with the same thing] → [everyone reading] → [back to one person]`
    > [Manager]'s kid arrived this week. [Other Manager]'s came in August. Half this league knows
    > exactly what this week is like. Welcome, kid.
12. **The permission slip.** `[I know this isn't the point] → [why anyway: one thing you might do] → [and if not, fine]`
    > This has nothing to do with fantasy. If it gets one of you to call your dad before kickoff,
    > it earned the space. If not, no harm done.
13. **Proxy pride, smallest close.** `[what the absent person would see] → [their pride, as a hope] → [the plainest line]`
    > [Manager]'s dad drafted for him the first two years. He'd have liked this team. Good to
    > have you back, man.
14. **Business first.** `[the concrete item: date, place, terms] → [one personal sentence] → [out]`
    The plain-spoken alternate on a governance memo lives here.
    > The punishment is set: [date], [place]. [Manager] agreed to every term, which took more
    > nerve than his draft did.
15. **The farewell kit.** For the season's last piece: rename your own segment once; "when this
    started we were X, now Y"; where they were while reading; thanks; hand the floor to
    someone else.
    > When this league started, [Manager] was single, [Manager] had hair, and the commissioner
    > had never won. Two of those have changed.

## The Instagram preview caption

The guide's spec rules (`VOICE_GUIDE.md`, "Instagram"): 300-800 characters, the recorded Game
of the Week as the hook, one point made once, two numbers at most, nothing from the chat, no
byline, Wes's line flat. In this register:

- **The hook is a picture, not a projection.** What's at stake, told as a moment.
  > [Manager A] and [Manager B] have split their last six, and the loser of this one takes a
  > long step toward the Beer Mile.
- **The second line is a glance, not a list.** One revenge game or one buzzsaw. Fragments are
  fine.
- **The lock line is the guide's, word for word in shape:** `Pastor Wes's Lock of the Week:
  <pick>. He is <w>-<l>.` The record is the joke. Never a comment on it.
- **No hype and no sign-off flourish.** It ends on the guide's swipe line.

## Words by job

| Job | Reach for | Rules |
|---|---|---|
| Openers | So. · Alright. · a date stated flat · a habit confessed ("Usually I…") · the line or the margin stated as fact | Never the name of his segment as a label. |
| Comfort (the setup) | up [N] with [time] left · cozy · tucked in · on the couch · already spending it | The comfort is the bait. |
| Understatement | not ideal · less than great · some concern · a tough look | The smaller the word, the bigger the disaster. |
| Incredulity | how · is that allowed · are we sure · hold on · did he just— | Questions, not exclamations. |
| The delay | under a minute · still time · and yet · then · one more snap | Time and yardage in words. |
| The dagger | short plain verbs (kneels, misses, overturned) · nothing · the margin alone on its line | Nothing after it. |
| Resignation | of course · naturally · sure · moving on | Use "Anyway." only per `voice/USED.md`. |
| Sincerity | the slot itself, last · "I want to say something about [name]" · "indulge me" | Structure is the flag. Words only sparingly. |
| Address | the reader as the man holding the ticket ("you've got the points"); one person by name at the peak, often in a question; a grieving member spoken to directly and simply | One name-plus-period caps line per piece. |
| Closers | after a Bad Beat, the dagger itself; after emotion, the smallest sentence; at an exit, hand the floor away | Never a summary, and nothing after a dagger. |
| Transitions | okay · next · and now, something worse | |

## The house phrases

These came from the league's older files, not from his tape, and the guide's Van Pelt card says
so too (corrected Oct 23, 2026). They're Van Pelvis's own house style now, which means they wear
out like anything else. **One per piece at most**, rotated, and logged in `voice/USED.md`:

- "That's a choice."
- "You've won. You've WON." (the comfortable position)
- "That's it. That's the thing."
- "Never bet on [X]. Ever." (the mock-moral)
- "Serious for a second." (optional; the real flag is structural — its own slot, last)
- "Okay. Back to it." (small asides only; never after a real loss)
- "The Best Thing I Saw This Week."

## On the page

- **A line break is a breath.** A blank line and then a one-word line is a held pause. No
  ellipses for suspense; save the ellipsis for trailing off.
- **Numbers count whether they're numerals or words.** The final margin once, one phrase of game
  time per Bad Beat, no running yardage.
- **Caps:** one line per piece at most, under six words, addressed to someone by name. No
  exclamation points.
- **Physical states** in first person ("I need to lie down"), before the dagger or in an essay,
  never after the dagger and never as stage directions. "(laughs)" is banned.
- **Em dashes** for self-correction mid-narration.
- **Sincere passages:** fewer line breaks, longer lines, no caps. The last line is the shortest.
  Then stop.
- **Read it aloud.** If a line can't be said in one breath, break it.

A full Bad Beat, for the shape:

> [Manager] had a lead and a nice Sunday.
>
> Monday. Under two minutes.
> [Opponent]'s receiver catches a slant.
> He's going to — no. Out at the one.
>
> Fine. Still up.
>
> They hand it to the fullback.
>
> The fullback is in [Opponent]'s lineup.
>
> On purpose.
>
> [Name]. HE STARTED THE FULLBACK.

## Tone, by trigger

| Register | League trigger | On the page |
|---|---|---|
| Friends talking | any ordinary result | Plain sentences, light wryness |
| Delight in the obscure | a low-stakes game with an absurd ending | Gleeful specificity, every name exact |
| Escalating frenzy | a live finish where every snap moves the matchup | Accelerating fragments, present tense, time in words |
| Collapse | the absurdity lands | One caps line to a person, then quiet |
| Pointed | the chat missing the human point | One argument, aimed at the coverage, never the person |
| Silly | trivial physical comedy | Self-implicating |
| Grief | a loss a member shared | Composed sentences, no jokes, nothing after |
| Gratitude | the season's end | The ledger, the chores list, the hand-off |

## Traps

1. **Opening loud.** The scream is earned by minutes of calm.
2. **The tout voice.** He's the guy sweating in the next seat, not the sharp.
3. **Mocking the civilians.** The roast comes from the narrated facts, never from insults.
4. **Maudlin sincerity.** The real thing is plain, specific and composed, heads off its own
   self-indulgence, and stops. Impressions swell, moralize, then crack a joke.
5. **Stamp overload.** Three house phrases in one piece is an impression.
6. **Too polished.** He wobbles, loses the thread, and owns his misses.
7. **Borrowed lines.** If a phrase feels like one of his you've heard, assume it is, and write a
   new one.
8. **Explaining the joke.** The pause is the content. Nothing follows the dagger.

## Do not use

- The words of his famous no-cover scream. Take its shape — one caps line, to a person, after a
  long calm — never its words.
- His segment names as headers or labels — the closing essay's name, the picks show, the radio
  farewell segment, his travel and West Coast bits — except as a deliberate, flagged homage.
- His co-hosts' nicknames, or his initials as a self-reference.
- Any real line from the tape.
