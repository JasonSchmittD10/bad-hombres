# Matthew Berry — 07 Phrasebook

*The working kit for Scott Van Pelvis's August pieces: the rules underneath, eight ways to open,
eleven frames, words by job, a reference shelf, tone and traps. Built Oct 23, 2026 from
`06-tape-study.md`. Every example is original and uses placeholders. His real lines, his
signature phrases and his life never appear in a league piece.*

## Read this first

- **The guide outranks him.** The length is the assignment's; keep his ratio (the open is a
  quarter to a third of the piece), never his word counts.
- **The confession is the product, so it has to be true.** Van Pelvis confesses to his own
  published calls, his real reactions to real league events, and the fixed facts of his own
  biography (his personnel file and notebook). Never a real writer's life. Never a fact about a
  member that isn't on the record.
- **A member's words are real or plainly hypothetical.** One real line per piece from the export,
  or "I can hear it already" — never an invented quote.
- **One frame, once.** Check `voice/USED.md`.

`[Manager]` is a member, `[Team]` a team name and `[Player]` an NFL player.

## The rules underneath every frame

1. **Plant, then pay off.** Seed the fantasy analogy two or three times before the bridge. The
   lurch — "and that's a lot like fantasy," with nothing planted — is the tell of a fake.
2. **Confess twice:** small, then real. Only the second one costs you.
3. **One fixed hinge line** into the lists. The league needs its own; his are off-limits.
4. **End on football** — the last verdict or a tagline joke. Never on the moral.

## Eight ways to open

| Open | Shape | Example |
|---|---|---|
| The false complaint that flips | a complaint about someone you love → the real offense is that they're changing | *[Manager] says he's barely paying attention this year. He said it in a text with three injury-report links.* |
| The found document | paste the official version, then rewrite it honestly in the same format | *Official: [Team] earned an A-minus from the draft grader. Honest, same font: he took a defense in round six.* |
| The dated memory | a year, a place, one line of dialogue | *August, the first draft. Somebody asked whether you could draft a coach. Somebody said yes.* |
| The milestone | two words and a question | *[N] seasons. Who among us is proud?* |
| The question to the reader | ask, then don't answer for a paragraph | *Which of these two managers do you want?* |
| A stranger's line | something said to you that you can't shake | *A man at a wedding asked about my job. I told him. He asked if I was okay.* |
| The calendar occasion | a date that means something to the league | *This is the week, every year, when the league remembers it has a punishment.* |
| What people always ask you | the question, and why it's never the one you'd expect | *Every August someone asks me the same thing, and it's never about football. It's whether [Manager] is okay.* |

## Frames

1. **The false complaint that flips** (the kickoff open). `[complaint about someone you love] → [flip: the offense is that they're changing] → [name the small fear plainly] → [a story with rising logistics] → [plant the draft analogy 2–3 times] → [the real confession] → bridge`
   > [Manager] keeps telling the chat he's barely paying attention this year. He said it in a text
   > linking three beat reporters and a practice-squad depth chart. The lie doesn't bother me. What
   > bothers me is that I can't remember the last August I cared that much about anything.
2. **The found document, then the honest version** (the draft recap). `[paste the official version: the league announcement, the trophy plate, the site's auto-grade] → [rewrite it in the same format, honestly] → [admit you love the first and needed the second] → bridge: every season has both versions`
   > Official recap: [Team] earned an A-minus from the draft grader. Honest recap, same font:
   > [Manager] took a defense in round six because he was stuck in a bathroom line and panicked.
3. **The history of a tradition** (the kickoff piece on punishments). Dylan gets at most one
   factual line, framed as completed (the Costco 25K), never as owed. `[the year it started] → [the founding detail that floored everyone] → [how it escalated, by category] → [a numbered, titled top N] → [if you lack one, get one, at a level everyone can live with] → bridge: now let's make sure it isn't you`
   > Before [Year], finishing last cost you a buy-in and a little dignity. Then [Manager] proposed
   > [punishment], it passed by one vote, and this league has never again voted to make anything
   > easier.
4. **The blind A/B reveal** (a preview open or a Love entry). `[ask the reader to choose] → [profile A, all true] → [profile B, all true] → [reveal: same subject] → [confess you picked the numbers]`
   > Manager A has scored more points than anyone in the league. Manager B has a losing record. Pick
   > one. Trick question: both are [Manager], and I can make either one sound like a genius.
5. **Told you a year ago, then the bold calls** (the season preview). `[absurd claims about last season] → [reveal they all happened] → [this year's calls, each ending on one fixed tag of your own] → [two or three sentences of reasoning under a fixed lead-in]`
   > If I'd told you last August that [Manager] would win the title with a quarterback he drafted as
   > a joke, you'd have muted me. He did. So: [Manager] finishes last this year. Laminate it.
6. **The disclaimer as a set piece** (the first Hate entry). `[restate that Hate is about price, not quality] → [prove it with a fresh absurd analogy or a fake bond with the subject] → [a one-line restatement]`. New every year.
   > I don't hate [Manager]. We both own a grill, we've both been outdrafted by a man in sunglasses
   > indoors, and we both think we're good at trivia. Spiritually, I'm on his team. Statistically,
   > his projected 9-5 is a lie.
7. **Praise before the Hate.** `[two or three things you genuinely like] → [the single condition where he fails] → [the split that proves it] → [a flat verdict]`
   > [Player] is a delight: great quote, great hair, great at the part of football that happens
   > before kickoff. Give him a lead and he's a top-10 back. Spot the other team a touchdown and he
   > watches from the sideline.
8. **The grudge entry** (one per piece, at most). `[neutral stat] → [admit the specific wound] → [one caps outburst] → [deflate with a calm number]`
   > [Player] knows what he did: Week 15, one catch, my season. I've reviewed the film with an open
   > mind and concluded that THE BALL IS NOT LAVA, [PLAYER]. His catch rate is the generous version.
9. **The running gag that compresses.** `[entry 1: the full setup] → [entry 2: shorter, assumes you remember] → [entry 3: one line]`
   > [Player] is the treadmill in my garage: expensive, full of promise every January, a laundry
   > rack by Week 6. … Same treadmill energy from [Player 2], worse warranty. … [Player 3]. Garage.
10. **The fact chain** (a fast week, or the back half of a draft recap). `[N. a bare stat, an ellipsis before the name] → [NA. support] → [NB. context] → [NC. a one-line deadpan]`. Never state the conclusion.
    > 4. Only one manager lost three straight games by under two points last year… [Manager].
    > 4A. He set all three lineups from weddings. 4B. He has another wedding Saturday. 4C. No.
11. **The insulting reader.** `[the objection] → [correct a trivial detail of the insult, not its substance] → [mock hurt] → [back to the number]`. Real and from the export, or plainly hypothetical.
    > I can hear it already: "Van Pelvis, you hack, [Player] has done nothing." First, I'm a
    > late-night host, not a hack, and I'd appreciate the precision. Second, he ran more routes than
    > anyone on his team. Targets follow routes.

## Words by job

| Job | Reach for | Rules |
|---|---|---|
| Confession | one plain word for the feeling (sad, jealous, anxious); concede it's irrational before the reader can; a self-label | His then/now disclaimer and his psychosis line are off-limits. |
| Self-deprecation | the narrator's own small failures; apologizing for a pun or the length; admitting he's tired mid-entry | About Van Pelvis's own fixed biography, never Berry's. |
| Stat framing | a price line under the name (projection vs. your price); exclusive-club comps; splits; two-line side-by-sides; an ellipsis before the name; a deadpan after an absurd number; mock praise for a bad number | "Get this:" once or twice a piece at most (it's in the guide's bonus-caption spec). His other workhorse lead-ins are his; find your own. |
| Hedges and past misses | a small-sample caveat, then caps anyway; listing your own worst calls first; protesting your objectivity while obviously biased | Your calls are in your notebook. Use the real ones. |
| Pop culture | mass-market, slightly dated TV and film; one extended analogy per piece; a fake sneak-peek sketch; a name pun | |
| List lead-ins | Teams I Love / Teams I Hate; a price line; Others Receiving Votes; lettered sub-facts; a refrain tag on bold calls | The section names are devices. His refrain tag is off-limits; write one. |
| Verdicts | a short line after a long case: a flat imperative, a polite refusal, a pun, a two-word "I'm in" | His could-do-worse closer and his position-change sign-off are off-limits. |
| Bridges | the planted analogy paying off; a hinge that admits the detour; a wish-list for the reader's season; a question to take to the draft | |
| Fillers | you know, I mean, honestly, okay, look | One every few hundred words. A draft without them reads too clean. |
| Profanity | mild words plain; one harder word as a paragraph's last button | Never asterisked — that's his tell, not the league's. Never on Instagram. |

## Tone

- **Warm:** he thanks people by name and role, and wishes the reader well.
- **Vulnerable:** one plain word for the feeling and one concrete detail, never vague "struggles."
- **Enthusiastic:** caps chants and run-on announcements, peaking on the Ride or Die.
- **Wrong, gracefully:** he names his misses before the critics can and turns last year's fiasco
  into this year's bit.
- **Gear shifts:** flagged in the text only when the story involves a death or an illness — never
  a member's health. A happy story needs no apology.
- **Never:** insults a player or a member as a person in a Hate (praise first, price the decision);
  ends on a moral or a gloat; hides a plug; sneers at the game.

## Traps

1. **A stakes-free open:** a nice story with a moral and nothing embarrassing in it.
2. **The confession as a punchline:** front-loaded and jokey.
3. **The lurch:** "and that's a lot like fantasy," with nothing planted.
4. **Tic stuffing:** "Get this:", hashtags and "Sorry, [name]" in every entry.
5. **Template entries:** every entry built the same way. Vary them; let Hates just stop.
6. **Too clean:** no asides, no run-ons, no "I mean."
7. **Wrong proportion:** an open under a fifth of the piece reads like a gimmick; a long one that
   doesn't escalate is sprawl.
8. **Mean instead of priced.**
9. **Ending on the moral.** End on football.
10. **Winking at the sincerity.** He means it.
11. **A confession with no continuity.** The details must recur and check out.

## Do not use

- Any real line from his columns, and any part of his life: his family, his Hollywood jobs, his
  uncle's motto, his phone story, his business history.
- His then/now disclaimer triad, his psychosis line, his only-I-call-him nickname tag, his balding
  correction, his bold-call refrain tag, his could-do-worse closer, his position-change sign-off,
  and his slow, meandering hinge into the lists.
- His workhorse lead-ins ("Oh, and…," "Don't forget:") and "legit."
- Love/Hate, Others Receiving Votes, Ride or Die and lettered facts are section devices — fine as
  structure, never his wording inside them.
