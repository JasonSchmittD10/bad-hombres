# Matthew Berry — 02 Column Architecture

## A. The Intro — the part the league copies
Beat by beat:

1. **Cold-open on a scene or a line of dialogue.** Not a thesis. A *moment*. Someone says something; a specific year and place. (His last one: a polite phone correction in 1995.)
2. **Widen the lens.** A paragraph of context — why the moment mattered to him.
3. **Confess.** The real feeling under the story — the slightly embarrassing one. Jealousy of a sitcom actor. Checking a score too many times. **This is the beat imitators skip and the one that makes it work.**
4. **Self-aware disclaimer.** "I realize that's crazy. I knew it then. I know it now." He names the absurdity before you can.
5. **The turn.** A second story, a quote, a death, a book excerpt — something that reframes the first story and raises the stakes.
6. **The tonal gear-shift, handled in-text.** "I don't mean to be frivolous… they aren't even in the same universe." He *tells* you he's about to go from serious to dumb, and asks permission.
7. **The bridge line.** One sentence connecting the personal theme to fantasy football. (*What do you hope they say about you? Approach your draft that way.*)
8. **"Let's get to it."** Hard cut.

The intro is the emotional investment. Everything after is cashing the check.

## B. Housekeeping
A short block of plugs, thank-yous, and programming notes in a slightly-apologetic-but-not-really tone: *And hey, it wouldn't be a Love/Hate without some plugs.* **He acknowledges the plug as a plug.** That's the move. League equivalent: dues / payouts / punishment reminders, done in the same voice.

## C. The Lists
**Quarterbacks I Love → Running Backs I Love → Pass Catchers I Love**, then the **Hate** versions. Each position gets full entries, then an **"Others receiving votes:"** run-on, then (for RBs) **"Late-round targets:"**.

### Micro-structure of one player entry
1. **Hook** (1–2 sentences). A joke, a fake objection (*I get the puzzling look on your face. Berry, you hate the Cowboys…*), or an absurd premise (*If there's a TE next year named Buccaroo McBucsalot…*).
2. **"Get this:" / "Here's the thing:"** → the stat pile. Three to six numbers, each with a comp or rank.
3. **The "so" sentence.** Plain-English translation. *So, to recap:* / *Look at it this way:*
4. **The callback button.** Return to the hook, escalated. (*Now excuse me while I blast Smash Mouth.*)

### Hate-entry DNA
Mandatory implicit disclaimer: hate ≠ bad player; hate = drafted too high / started too often. Often closes with a polite *Sorry, DK.* or a mock suggestion (*Maybe consider a position change?*).

## D. "Others receiving votes"
Ellipsis-chained paragraphs: *… Speaking of X, how about Y? … From Bill to Will Shipley. … From wood to Woody Marks.* The chain-link transitions ("Speaking of," "From X to Y," "Along with X, my most-drafted…") let him cover fifteen players in one breath while it feels like a stream of thought. **This is the fix for a flat "The Rest" section.**

## E. Recurring devices inside the lists
- **Ride or Die** — one annual pick, announced with ceremony, referenced all year.
- **Running gag threaded across entries** — smelling salts for Kittle, then "pick them back up" for Pitts.
- **Fake reader objections** — staged and answered.
- **The stat-that-isn't** — *LeSean McCoy is no longer in the NFL (so you should not draft him … hashtag: analysis!)*.

## F. 100 Facts / 10 Facts (the stripped form)
Numbered facts, each a self-contained stat + one-line implication + joke. No intro story by rule. His current column as of 2025–26. Model for a **fast weekly awards/stats post** when a full narrative wrap-up isn't warranted.

```
[CLOSER — callback to intro theme; thank someone]
```
