# Matthew Berry — 02 Column Architecture

## A. The Intro — the part the league copies
Beat by beat:

1. **Cold-open on a scene or a line of dialogue.** Not a thesis. A *moment*. Someone says something; a specific year and place. (His last one: a polite phone correction in 1995.)
2. **Widen the lens.** A paragraph of context — why the moment mattered to him.
3. **Confess.** The real feeling under the story — the slightly embarrassing one. Jealousy of a sitcom actor. Checking a score too many times. **This is the beat imitators skip and the one that makes it work.**
4. **Self-aware disclaimer.** He names the absurdity before you can. (His own then/now wording is off-limits; write a new one every time.)
5. **The turn.** A second story, a quote, a death, a book excerpt — something that reframes the first story and raises the stakes.
6. **The tonal gear-shift, handled in-text.** When the story involves a death or an illness, he *tells* you he's about to go from serious to dumb, and says outright that the two aren't comparable.
7. **The bridge line.** One sentence connecting the personal theme to fantasy football. (*Original:* Every season ends with somebody explaining what went wrong. Draft like you're writing that explanation now.)
8. **"Let's get to it."** Hard cut.

The intro is the emotional investment. Everything after is cashing the check.

## B. Housekeeping
A short block of plugs, thank-yous, and programming notes in a slightly-apologetic-but-not-really tone. **He acknowledges the plug as a plug.** That's the move. League equivalent: dues / payouts / punishment reminders, done in the same voice.

## C. The Lists
**Quarterbacks I Love → Running Backs I Love → Pass Catchers I Love**, then the **Hate** versions. Each position gets full entries, then an **"Others receiving votes:"** run-on, then (for RBs) **"Late-round targets:"**.

### Micro-structure of one player entry
1. **Hook** (1–2 sentences). A joke, a fake objection (*original:* I can hear it already: "You hate my team." I don't. I hate the price.), or an absurd premise one step past a real fact.
2. **"Get this:" / "Here's the thing:"** → the stat pile. Three to six numbers, each with a comp or rank.
3. **The "so" sentence.** Plain-English translation. *So, to recap:* / *Look at it this way:*
4. **The callback button.** Return to the hook, escalated. (Most of his entries actually skip it; see `06-tape-study.md`.)

### Hate-entry DNA
Mandatory implicit disclaimer: hate ≠ bad player; hate = drafted too high / started too often. Often just stops, or closes on a polite apology to the player. (His position-change sign-off is off-limits.)

## D. "Others receiving votes"
Ellipsis-chained paragraphs: *… [an item] … [an item] …*, each two to five sentences. Occasionally a chain-link transition ("speaking of," "from X to Y") joins two items; the ellipsis is the constant. It lets him cover fifteen players in one breath while it feels like a stream of thought. **This is the fix for a flat "The Rest" section.**

## E. Recurring devices inside the lists
- **Ride or Die** — one annual pick, announced with ceremony, referenced all year.
- **Running gag threaded across entries** — one setup that comes back shorter each time.
- **Fake reader objections** — staged and answered.
- **The stat-that-isn't** — a fact so obvious it's a joke, delivered as analysis.

## F. 100 Facts / 10 Facts (the stripped form)
Numbered facts, each a self-contained stat + one-line implication + joke. No intro story by rule. His current column as of 2025–26. Model for a **fast weekly awards/stats post** when a full narrative wrap-up isn't warranted.

## G. Template — full Love/Hate shape (copy-paste)
```
[INTRO: scene → widen → confess → disclaim → turn → gear-shift → bridge]

Let's get to it.

[HOUSEKEEPING — plugs/reminders, acknowledged as such]

[POSITION] I Love
  [Entry: hook → "Get this:" stats → "so" → callback]
  Others receiving votes: … Speaking of … From X to Y …
[POSITION] I Hate
  [Entry: hook → stats → "so" → "Sorry, name."]

[CLOSER — callback to intro theme; thank someone]
```
