# Matthew Berry — 06 Tape Study

*Eight columns and four short forms, 2016–2025, broken down by how they're built. Researched
Oct 23, 2026. This is Scott Van Pelvis's August register: the draft recap, the season preview
and the kickoff piece. Every description is a paraphrase and every example in this folder is an
original composition. Study the shape and never borrow a line.*

**How this was built:** twelve columns read in full (about 74,000 words; three from ESPN,
2016–2020, nine from NBC, 2023–2025) and six secondary sources. Word counts come from text
extraction and include some captions, so treat them as ±10%. No podcast transcripts surfaced.
Links are at the bottom.

---

## The columns

### 1. Preseason Love/Hate, Aug 2016 — the found document
- **Open:** he reprints the network's real press release about his new contract, then writes a
  mock release in the same corporate voice listing every firing, cancelled segment and bad call
  of his career, plus real posts from his critics.
- **Confession:** the mock release is the confession — a failure résumé — followed by one sincere
  paragraph about having worked every year since he was a teenager.
- **Bridge:** every season produces both versions — the official one and the honest one — and
  the winners live through the second.
- **Lists:** Love then Hate by position, his rank beside the market price. One Hate entry is a
  whole category.
- **Gags:** the new contract makes him "untouchable," so he refuses to write new material and
  reprints an old entry as a laziness bit. A running gag about his hair gets shorter each time.
  One grudge entry goes to grawlix caps over a receiver who cost him a playoff game.
- **Steal:** the intro sets up a persona the entries keep playing for the whole column, and
  quoting his own haters makes the brag safe.

### 2. Week 1 Love/Hate, Sept 2020 — the history of a tradition
- **Open:** a dated memory. Years earlier, researching a book, he went hunting for last-place
  punishments, and a reader sent in a league whose loser gets a tattoo the winner picks. The shock
  lands in one-line paragraphs and caps.
- **Body:** how he promoted that league everywhere, a winking claim that he made punishments a
  thing, a catalog of punishment genres, then a numbered top ten, each with a joke title.
- **Confession:** almost none. The readers' leagues are the heroes.
- **Bridge:** if your league has no punishment, vote one in before Sunday, at a level everyone can
  live with — then let's make sure it's never you.
- **Steal:** the topic is the reader's own league. **This is the closest real template for a Bad
  Hombres kickoff piece.**

### 3. Week 13 Love/Hate, Dec 2020 — the tribute
- **Open:** a calendar occasion (a cancer-charity week and a late colleague's day). He admits he's
  retelling a story few people read the first time.
- **Story:** three vignettes — the colleague vouching for him when he was nobody; steering him away
  from a bar confrontation before it became a headline (the embarrassing middle one); bringing it up
  years later in front of family. Then the last time he saw him.
- **Confession:** he was a nobody and nearly threw a punch. They were never close, and that's the
  point.
- **Bridge:** his annual charity offer, then the honored man's own on-air introduction of him used
  as the line into the lists.
- **Steal:** the honored person's words become the bridge, and the charity ask carries a joke, so
  the tribute never turns into a sermon.

### 4. Preseason Love/Hate, Aug 2023 — the false complaint
- **Open:** a complaint that his daughters won't obey him, which flips within a paragraph: what
  they refuse is his plea to stop growing up. One fragment per line.
- **Confession, in two sizes:** early, he names the feeling — sad, anxious, the clueless sitcom
  dad. Much later, stuck in a flight delay, he admits he'd decided one concert would settle his
  relationship with them, and calls that irrational.
- **Seeding:** he likens the concert to draft day twice before the bridge, and the payoff comes
  when one daughter says something small that means everything.
- **Bridge:** a hinge that jokes about how long that took, the annual disclaimer, self-mocking
  plugs, then a short wish-list for the reader's season that echoes the concert.
- **Steal:** specific detail, rising logistics, and joy that lands because he named the fear
  early.

### 5. Preseason Love/Hate, Aug 2024 — the listicle that's a memoir
- **Open:** a two-word anniversary fragment and a question, then the origin story: a successful
  friend told him to go out on his own, promised to teach him how, and never did.
- **Body:** a numbered list of ten business lessons that double as fantasy advice.
- **Confession:** he knew nothing about business, picked a terrible web address, stayed loyal too
  long and got burned. He still makes mistakes, just never the same one twice.
- **Turn:** a late uncle's never-satisfied motto, applied to a big career decision.
- **Steal:** a list intro can be useful and personal at once, and a motto planted one year can
  come back the next.

### 6. Week 1 Love/Hate, Sept 2024 — no story at all
- **Open:** a paragraph of absurd-sounding claims about the previous season, then the reveal that
  every one came true. Then ten bold predictions, each ending on the same refrain tag, each with a
  fixed lead-in for the reasoning.
- **Steal:** an in-season piece doesn't have to be memoir. A list with a refrain can fill the
  intro slot.

### 7. Preseason Love/Hate, Aug 2025 — the final one
- **Open:** a line of dialogue: decades ago, he gently told strangers on the phone that he wasn't
  the famous actor with nearly the same name. Then the context — his small Hollywood jobs, walking
  past the other man's billboard on the same studio lot.
- **Confession:** strangers' disappointment made him feel he'd failed them, and he was jealous of
  a man who didn't know he existed. He calls it irrational.
- **Turn:** the actor's death, and a passage from the actor's memoir about what he hoped his work
  would be remembered for. He says outright that he isn't comparing addiction to fake football.
- **Bridge:** what people will say about him against what he hopes they'll say, and why the column
  is ending. The reader gets the question: what would you want said about you?
- **Steal:** a genuinely embarrassing confession, stakes raised by a death, the gear shift
  handled, and his own decision handed to the reader as a question.

### 8. 100 Facts, Aug 2025 — admitting the trick
- **Open:** he asks the reader to choose between two players, gives two blind profiles, then
  reveals that both are the same player.
- **Confession:** he can make numbers argue anything, so the facts that follow are true but chosen
  to support his opinions.
- **Facts:** short and bare. Lettered sub-facts build a case, an ellipsis holds back the surprising
  name, and the kicker lines are exasperated questions or deadpans. The last fact is the punchline
  of the last chain.
- **Steal:** admitting the trick buys license to be one-sided a hundred times.

### Short forms
- **A draft-day manifesto (2025):** opens on the thing people tell him that he loves most, then a
  ring-announcer welcome, habits laid out like a self-help book, the same question repeated after
  each stat set, and a tease for a sequel.
- **10 Facts (2025):** under 1,200 words by his own rule, no story, written in a workday — though
  both sampled editions still open with a few hundred words of non-story preamble.
- **10 Lists of 10 (2024):** plugs first, then themed list titles, some with a one-paragraph
  anecdote.

## The numbers

- **Intro length:** preseason intros ran about a quarter to a third of a 10,000–12,000-word column.
  In-season intros were shorter, and some had no story.
- **Confessions:** the first admission lands several hundred words in, and the bigger one about
  three-quarters of the way through the intro. Story-led intros plant the fantasy analogy two or
  three times before the bridge.
- **Housekeeping:** a few hundred words, plugs flagged as plugs, before the lists.
- **Entries:** Love entries run longer than Hate entries, and the Hates keep shrinking through the
  last position group.
- **Others Receiving Votes:** the longest paragraphs in the piece, items of two to five sentences
  split by ellipses.
- **For the league: keep the ratio, not the length.** The length is the assignment's.

## Patterns across the twelve

1. **Plant, then pay off.** The analogy is seeded before the bridge, never announced at it.
2. **Confess twice:** small, then real. Only the second one costs him.
3. **One fixed hinge line** into the lists.
4. **End on football** — the last verdict or a tagline joke — never on the moral.
5. **Entries vary.** Joke first, stat first, a sketch, a fake objection; many Hates just stop.
6. **The intro's theme leaks into the entries,** and his biography stays consistent from year to
   year, so the details can be checked.

## Corrections to the older files in this folder

- **His real lines were in our files.** `02`, `03` and `04` carried several of his actual lines or
  close rewrites — his then/now disclaimer, his gear-shift line, his bridge question from 2025,
  team-nickname and band-name jokes, a caps line about a tight end, his position-change sign-off,
  chain-link examples, and a stage direction from his phone story. All were replaced with
  originals on Oct 23, 2026. Never put them back.
- **"Every Love/Hate opened with a 500–2,500-word story" (`01`):** a Week 1 column had none, and the
  sampled preseason intros ran longer.
- **"Confess in the first 300 words" (`05`):** the first admission lands later, and a bigger one
  comes after it.
- **The closer (`02` §G, `04`, `05`):** in most sampled columns the thanks come before the lists and
  the callback sits in the bridge. The NBC columns simply end on the last entry.
- **"Get this:" and a callback in every entry (`02`, `05`):** "Get this:" appears zero to five times a
  column, and most entries have no callback. His real workhorses are "Oh, and" and "Don't forget."
- **Others Receiving Votes (`02` §D):** the ellipsis is the constant. Explicit "speaking of" links
  are rare, and items run several sentences.
- **10 Facts (`02` §H, `04`):** the real facts are bare and chained with lettered sub-facts, the
  conclusions are often left unstated, and jokes are rationed to the kickers.
- **"No profanity, only asterisks" (`03`):** mild words appear plain; asterisks are for harder ones.
- **His fillers (`03`):** "Dude," "Let's be honest" and "Truth is" barely appear. The real ones are
  "you know," "I mean," "honestly" and "legit."
- **Pre-guide instructions that no longer hold:** the commissioner as narrator in `04`'s samples, and
  a punishment line about Dylan. The guide overrides both.

## For Scott Van Pelvis (August) — the guide comes first

- **Length is the assignment's.** Keep Berry's ratio — the open is a quarter to a third of the
  piece — never his 10,000 words.
- **Confess checkable things.** Berry's details recur and can be checked; Van Pelvis's must too.
  His confessions come from his own published calls (the notebook has them), his real reactions to
  real league events, and the fixed facts of his biography. Never a real writer's life, and never
  a fact about a member that isn't on the record.
- **The fake reader** is a member only when the line is real and from the export; otherwise it's
  plainly hypothetical ("I can hear it already").
- **The kickoff piece has a real template** (column 2): the history of the league's punishments,
  told by category, ending on "let's make sure it isn't you." Dylan gets at most one factual
  line, framed as completed, never as owed (the guide: his punishments are not a theme).
