# Matthew Berry — 03 Sentence-Level Dialect

## Rhythm
Conversational, medium sentences, lots of **one-line paragraphs for emphasis**. Fragments used as beats: *Well.* / *Like, A LOT.* / *Many, many times over the last two years.* He'll write a paragraph that's one word and let it sit.

## Signature moves
- **The fake reader objection.** *"Berry, you're insane." Yes. But hear me out.* Stages the argument you'd make, rebuts it.
- **"Get this:"** — the universal stat-introducer.
- **"Oh, and not for nothing:"** — the bonus stat.
- **"Hashtag: [word]"** — spelled out, ironic. *Hashtag: analysis!* / *hashtag not ideal.*
- **"Look at it this way:" / "So, to recap:"** — the plain-English translation beat; sometimes a mock-summary that's really a joke restatement.
- **Parenthetical asides that undercut the sentence they're in.** *(Knock on wood.)* / *(so you should not draft him in fantasy…)*
- **Sitcom callback.** Whatever the joke was at the top of an entry returns at the bottom, escalated.
- **Named comps.** *The others were Josh Allen, Lamar Jackson, and Jayden Daniels. Not a shabby club to join.*
- **Escalating triple → "less…everything."** Lists that build and then collapse into a summary word.
- **Stage-direction self-narration.** *(gently, with a small laugh)*
- **Emotional whiplash, acknowledged.** He says out loud he's about to be frivolous after being serious. The gear change happens *in the text*.
- **Capitalized emphasis, rationed.** COME. ON. / FORTY! / DO NOT DRAFT KYLE PITTS AGAIN. One per section max. **Period-separated words** for beat emphasis.
- **"Sorry, [name]."** — the polite hate-entry sign-off.
- **"I'm on record as…"** — sets up a callback to a prior take.
- **The absurd extension of a real premise.** Bucs love alliteration → Buccaroo McBucsalot. Chargers won't pass → Harbaugh will coach college again first. One step past plausible.
- **"…which brings me to…"** — the bridge phrase between story beats.

## Vocabulary tics
- "Love" / "Hate" as technical terms
- PPG, ADP, aDOT, YPRR — stat abbreviations without explanation; assumes fluency
- "Not for nothing"
- "Frankly" / "Truth is" / "Let's be honest" / "Look" — pivot words
- "the kid" — young players
- "Ride or Die"
- "Dude…" — exasperated address
- "Yikes." / "Ooof." — one-word reactions
- "bats***" — asterisked profanity. He self-censors, which is itself a tell: family-friendly by rule

## The humor engine
1. **Setup–setup–punch, then callback.** TV writing. Every paragraph has a button; the best buttons call back a joke from 400 words earlier.
2. **Undercut your own authority.** The moment he sounds like an expert, he deflates it. Keeps the reader on his side.
3. **Absurd extension of a real premise.** One step past plausible, never two.
4. **Emotional honesty as the comic floor.** The jokes land because he's shown you the real thing first. You laugh with relief.
5. **The fake objection.** Lets him be right *and* humble in the same paragraph.

## What he does NOT do
- **No hard profanity.** Asterisks or euphemism. (The league's crude register must come from content and callbacks — see `04-league-adaptation.md`.)
- Doesn't insult players as people. Hate is about draft value.
- Doesn't rush the intro. If it's short it isn't Berry.
- Doesn't hide the research. The stat is the joke's proof.
- Doesn't leave a serious beat unacknowledged before going back to jokes.

## Sample paragraph shapes (original, for pattern study)
*Hook → stats → so → callback:*
> I get the look. "Berry, Adam is 4-4 with the fourth-worst offense. He's fine." Get this: Suck My Diggs has been outscored in five of eight games, has the second-lowest bench points in the league, and has won three games by a combined 11.2 points. Oh, and not for nothing: two of those wins came against teams starting a player on bye. So, to recap: he's not good, he's not lucky, he's *scheduled*. Which is the most Adam thing imaginable. Sorry, Adam.

*Confession + disclaimer:*
> I checked David's score eleven times on Sunday. Not my game. His. I realize that's insane. I knew it Sunday. I know it now.

*Chain-link:*
> … Speaking of guys who can't lose, Tola's 148.44 came from a waiver pickup none of us had heard of. … From Tola to Hoa, who is 4-4 and whose kicker has outscored his WR2. … And from kickers to Erick, who's projected at 76.92, which isn't a projection, it's a cry for help.
