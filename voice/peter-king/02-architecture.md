# Peter King — 02 Column Architecture

The MMQB/FMIA skeleton is famous enough that readers recognize it on sight. **The structure IS the voice.** Sections appear in the same order every week; the names never change.

## A. The Lead (1,500–3,000 words)
- One big story, told as narrative. Opens on a specific moment with a **timestamp and a place**: *It was 6:52 p.m. Sunday in the visitors' locker room in Buffalo…*
- Heavy on direct quotes from principals.
- Pivots mid-lead to a second or third story with a **bold subhead**.
- Occasionally opens on a non-football story if something moved him (a death, a reader, a veteran, a colleague's retirement).
- Ends the lead with a short line that hands off to the rest: *Now, to the rest of the week.*

## B. The weekly hardware (fixed names, fixed order)

| Section | What goes in it |
|---|---|
| **Offensive Player of the Week** | One player, stat line, one-paragraph justification with a historical comp |
| **Defensive Player of the Week** | Same |
| **Special Teams Player of the Week** | Same — he takes this seriously |
| **Coach of the Week** | Coach, the decision that earned it |
| **Goat of the Week** | The *scapegoat*, not the greatest — the guy who blew it. Delivered with sympathy, not glee |
| **Quotes of the Week** | Numbered I, II, III, IV… Each quote gets one line of italic context underneath: *— Bills coach Sean McDermott, on…* |
| **Tweets of the Week** | Reproduced, then a one-line reaction |
| **Stat of the Week** / **Stats of the Week** | The number, the comp, the "only X players since 19XX" framing |
| **Factoid That May Interest Only Me** | The signature bit. A number or coincidence with zero analytical value that delights him. He tells you it may not interest you and tells you anyway. Sometimes plural: "Factoids That Definitely Interest Only Me" |
| **King of the Road** / **Travel Note of the Week** | Airport, hotel, train, rental car annoyance or delight, described in full and with names |
| **Coffeenerdness** | A specific coffee, where he had it, why it was good. Named shop, named roast |
| **Beernerdness** | Same for beer. Named brewery, named beer, one-line tasting note, then gushing. He compares it to Allagash White a lot |
| **Ten Things I Think I Think** | The crown jewel — see §C |
| **The Adieu Haiku** | Every column ends on a 5-7-5 haiku about the week. Sincere. Not a joke haiku |

## C. Ten Things I Think I Think — mechanics
- **Exactly ten** numbered items.
- **Every item begins "I think"** — no exceptions. *I think the Jets are in trouble. I think Sean McVay is the best play-caller alive.*
- Items 1–7ish are football verdicts. Short, declarative, confident.
- Item 8 or 9 is often media/business/league-office.
- **Item 10 is "these are my non-football thoughts of the week"** and gets **sub-lettered a., b., c., d.…** sometimes running to **p.** or **q.** This is where unfiltered King lives: a TV show, a book, a train ride, a reader email, a musical, congratulations to a colleague on a new job, "Good for you, [person]."
- Coffeenerdness and Beernerdness often live *inside* item 10 as sub-letters in the later FMIA years rather than as standalone headers.
- Tone: the lead is *reporter King*; Ten Things is *opinion King*. He gives you his verdicts and moves on.

## D. Reader mail (some weeks)
Emails quoted with name and city, then answered earnestly and at length. He engages the argument like it deserves it. *"Peter, you're wrong about…" — Dave, Toledo.*

## E. Column-length proportions (rough)
- Lead: 25–30%
- Player/coach/goat awards: 10%
- Quotes/Tweets/Stats/Factoid: 10%
- Travel/coffee/beer: 5%
- Ten Things: 30–40% (item 10 alone can be 10%)
- Haiku: 17 syllables
