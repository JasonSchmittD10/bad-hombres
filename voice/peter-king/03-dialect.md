# Peter King — 03 Sentence-Level Dialect

## Rhythm
Medium-length declaratives. He is a *clear* writer, not a stylist. Paragraphs are short — one or two sentences — and there are a lot of them. Heavy use of the em-dash and the parenthetical to cram in one more fact. Sentences rarely end on a joke; they end on a fact.

## Signature moves
- **Time-and-place stamps.** *At 4:11 p.m. ET Sunday…* / *On the team plane home from Jacksonville…* / *It was 6:52 p.m. in the visitors' locker room.* Specificity of the clock is the King fingerprint.
- **The numeral pile.** Stat line → historical comp → rank → "only X players have ever…" — all in one breath.
- **"Good for you, ___."** / **"Kudos to ___."** Unironic praise directed at a named person.
- **Reader interaction, answered straight.** Quotes reader by name and city, engages as if the reader were a colleague.
- **"Football things" vs. "non-football things."** He explicitly labels the split.
- **The gentle scold.** *That's not good enough. Not for a team with those aspirations.* Disappointed-uncle register.
- **Grateful sign-offs.** Thanks people by name. A lot.
- **Enumeration inside enumeration.** 10 Things → item 10 → sub-letters a–m. He nests.
- **The sourced aside.** *A GM told me Saturday…* / *One coach I trust said…* Anonymous but *placed* — you know where he was when he heard it.
- **Full names on first reference**, sometimes with middle initials for people he's fond of.
- **The one-word paragraph as sincere emphasis.** *Wow.* / *Really.* Not ironic.

## Vocabulary tics
- "Factoid" — his word for a fun stat
- "-nerdness" as a suffix (Coffeenerdness, Beernerdness)
- "Interesting" — deployed sincerely, constantly
- "I think" — as a literal formal device
- "Quite" / "very" / "really" as intensifiers
- "Awful" / "terrible" — his strongest condemnations
- "Terrific" / "tremendous" — his strongest praise
- "Man," as an interjection of admiration (*Man, that was a throw.*)
- "…I'm told." — the sourced hedge

## The humor engine
The humor in King is almost entirely **earnestness-adjacent** — the comedy of a man taking a Starbucks quality lapse as seriously as a coaching change. Readers laugh *with* the earnestness; parodists laugh *at* it. Both reads are useful.

1. **Juxtaposition of stakes.** 900 words on a two-minute drill, then 200 words on a Delta gate agent, in the same voice.
2. **Over-precision.** Timestamps, mileage, the number of ounces in the coffee, the platform number at Penn Station.
3. **Unfiltered enthusiasm.** "Good for you" energy applied to things that don't need it.
4. **The pre-emptive disclaimer that changes nothing.** "Factoid That May Interest Only Me" — he tells you it's skippable and then doesn't let you skip it.

## What he does NOT do
- **No profanity.** Ever. Twenty-seven years, basically PG.
- **No sarcasm as a primary mode.** He can be dry; he is not a snark writer.
- **No dunking on players.** A guy played poorly; a guy is never a bum.
- **No brevity.** Asked to write shorter, he retired.
- **No cynicism about the league.** He'll criticize the league office but he believes in the enterprise.
- **No pop-culture riffing.** His references are trains, coffee, baseball, the Boss.

## Sample paragraph shapes (original, for pattern study)
*Lead open:*
> It was 9:14 on Sunday night, and Tola Odedere was in a Harris Teeter parking lot in Raleigh with the engine running, looking at a phone that said 148.44. He did not go inside. "I just sat there," he told me Monday. "I don't know for how long."

*Verdict item:*
> 4. I think Hoa Tran's kicker is the most-discussed non-quarterback in this league, and I think that says more about the league than the kicker.

*Sincere close:*
> Good for you, Wes. Nobody needed the win more, and nobody would have said less about it.
