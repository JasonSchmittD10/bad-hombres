# Peter King — 06 Tape Study

*Nine real columns, 2013–2024, broken down by how they're built. Researched Oct 23, 2026. Every
description here is a paraphrase and every example line is an original composition — study the
shape, never borrow a sentence. Wolf Blitzed writes in this register when he's sober.*

**Caveat on the sources:** the Feb 26, 2024 farewell was read in full; the other eight came through
a summarizing reader, so treat their finer details as likely, not certain. Links are at the bottom.

---

## The columns

### 1. Championship Sunday, Jan 2013 (SI) — two games, one spine
- **Open:** a dateline and one flat declaration, a flashback to an older playoff game, then a
  late-night scene stamped to the minute: the winning team's buses at an airport, built around a
  single line from the coach.
- **Reporting:** the locker room, a hotel lobby, the phone, a colleague's research, an analytics
  guy. Tiny physical details — something signed in a tunnel, a remark from a bellman.
- **Back half:** a ranking, then an umbrella awards section that also held the stats, the factoid
  and a travel gripe (a lost cab driver while he was on the phone with a GM). Sections renamed
  that week in tribute to a legend who had just died.
- **Why it worked:** two stories, one thread (coaching brothers). The haiku ends deadpan.
- **Steal:** find the single thread that ties two unrelated results together.

### 2. A December Ten Things page, 2017 (SI) — range inside one list
- Item 1 is itself a lettered list of quick takes. Items 2–8 run from gentle (a GM search) to
  blunt (concussion handling, with a call for an investigation). Item 9 plugs a colleague. Item 10
  runs a to t: a coffee and the bottle that keeps it hot, a craving for cheap lager over craft,
  "of the Week" reading picks, an obituary, birthdays, a movie, one dry jab, a plea to save a
  newspaper.
- **Why it worked:** the register moves — blunt on safety, soft on people, exactly one ironic
  beat — and the format holds all of it.
- **Steal:** let one list carry very different temperatures, with exactly one wink.

### 3. Leaving SI, May 2018 — the news, then every section anyway
- **Open:** no scene. First person, he announces he's leaving and frames it as a handoff: age,
  family history, the news cycle, young writers who need the room.
- **Then:** the full skeleton regardless — draft leftovers, contract math, a call to act on how a
  group of workers was treated, kickoff analysis, a farewell to a retiring local columnist. The
  last sub-letter thanks readers.
- **Why it worked:** the biggest personal news of his career, and he still files every section.
  The loyalty to the format is the emotion.
- **Steal:** on a big week, do the routine anyway. The routine says it for you.

### 4. Embedded with a team, Nov 2018 (FMIA) — access, stamped to the minute
- **Open:** a coach addresses his team in a named hotel ballroom on a Saturday night, King at the
  back table, narrated hour by hour.
- **Texture:** clothes, a player's hidden talent, the coach staring at nothing. He tells you how
  the access was arranged and that nobody else had it.
- **Handoffs:** a labeled intermission explains the unusual format, then separate headers.
- **Back half:** two Goats, criticized, blame spread around. Item 10 runs to x.
- **Why it worked:** fly-on-the-wall, with the reporter visible but small.
- **Steal:** the reporter is in the room and almost never the subject.

### 5. A star's return, Oct 2021 (FMIA) — the biggest story from three feet away
- **Open:** dateline, an approximate small-hours time, the star ducking into a side room, his
  first words short and tired.
- **Reporting:** rain, a fan in costume, coaches and a backup, placed anonymous team sources, and
  ordinary fans from nearby states, each tagged with a job.
- **Handoffs:** a "The Lead" header with the subject, then two labeled mini-leads (a name and a
  verdict).
- **Back half:** several award winners, tweets each tagged with who the tweeter is, corrections in
  a mailbag section, a long item 10.
- **Steal:** start close, then widen patiently.

### 6. A shootout, Oct 2022 (FMIA) — the odd detail is the doorway
- **Open:** second person. You're in the visitors' hallway before anyone is named; the score and
  the game's length are the first hard facts.
- **Hook:** a humble, funny detail from a star's college days explains who he is now — and the
  headline sells the detail, not the score.
- **Back half:** a quick-hit list, a tidbits block, a guest essay, an award for an
  under-the-radar player, a community-service award, a myth-check on a number, a factoid about
  near-identical names. No coffee, no beer that week.
- **Steal:** headline the strange true detail, not the result.

### 7. A grave week, Jan 2023 (FMIA) — precision instead of adjectives
- **Open:** a what-if, then the opening play under a dateline; the clock arrives later.
- **Grief:** room numbers, mileage, times. Other people carry the feeling — teammates, a friend
  who drove to the hospital, a shop owner, a league doctor.
- **Pivot:** an explicit back-to-normal line, then something dry; he grades his own preseason
  picks to bridge to the lighter material. The closing verse apologizes to someone he'd doubted.
- **Why it worked:** he lets the facts be emotional so he doesn't have to be.
- **Steal:** when it's serious, get more exact, not more adjectival — and say out loud when you're
  going back to the game.

### 8. The farewell, Feb 26, 2024 (read in full)
- **Open:** an italic interior thought during Super Bowl week, then gratitude, the news, and four
  reasons as bold run-ins — interest, other lives, length (NBC asked him to cut by about 40
  percent and he couldn't), family.
- **Proof:** he demonstrates the length problem with a 3 a.m. stat obsession that became an item
  in that same column.
- **Middle:** thanks with exact fundraiser totals, rivals recommended by name, worries and hopes
  for the business, a parting gift (how a famous play was invented).
- **Back half:** favorite moments tagged by date and place, a worst-predictions list, a ranked
  nice-guys list split into divisions, career quotes where the attribution line carries the joke,
  identical-twin stat lines, odd factoids, a long bus trip narrated highway by highway, and a
  last Ten Things that thanks everyone and runs item 10 from a to z.
- **Why it worked:** the format delivers its own eulogy.
- **Steal:** a sign-off season piece can be built entirely from the column's own furniture.

### 9. The real finale, Mar 4, 2024 — the readers get the last word
- Opens on a colleague's death, then hands the column to reader letters, grouped under geography
  headers, each with name and town, his replies brief and gracious. He prints the ribbing about
  his chain-coffee loyalty.
- **Steal:** let the audience end it.

## Patterns across the nine

1. **Precision is the comedy and the emotion.** Times to the minute, room-level places, exact
   totals. The same tool makes a waiver claim funny and a grave week bearable. (For Wolf it's
   rooms and roads, not minutes. See the last section.)
2. **The reporter is present and small.** "I was at the back table." Never the hero.
3. **Criticism names a decision, shows the numbers, shares the blame, and ends short.** It is never
   about a man's character.
4. **Format loyalty.** Every section shows up, every week, whatever happened.
5. **One wink per list.** Sustained sarcasm never; a single dry beat, yes.
6. **The back half is a curated reading list,** not a junk drawer — "of the Week" picks, causes,
   obituaries, thank-yous.
7. **He grades himself.** Preseason picks, worst takes, corrections, apologies in verse.

## Corrections to the older files in this folder

The research doesn't support a few things `02`–`05` say. Where they disagree, trust this file:

- **"Good for you, ___"** isn't in the farewell and wasn't reported in any of the other eight. It's
  a house device, not his load-bearing tic. Rotate praise instead (see `07-phrasebook.md`).
- **Coffee and beer** live as lettered sub-items inside item 10, not as their own headers — and
  some weeks they don't appear at all. Optional, never required.
- **The Goat** is criticized fairly — specific, blame shared — not handled with sympathy.
- **The haiku** is straight-faced but its last line is often a dry kicker; it's fully sincere only
  on grave or farewell weeks.
- **"Now, to the rest of the week"** never appeared. FMIA hands off with labeled headers, and on
  grave weeks he states the pivot outright. (It's retired for the league anyway — `voice/USED.md`.)
- **Pop culture and mild exclamations** do appear: sitcoms, films, classic rock, sincerely; "heck,"
  "Lord," a rare italic *holy crap*. Profanity inside quotes gets dashed out.
- **Item 10** usually runs to s–z, not p/q.
- **The farewell** (Feb 26) was the announcement and last regular column; the true finale (Mar 4)
  was reader letters. Length was one of four reasons, not the whole story.

## For Wolf Blitzed (sober) — the reset comes first

King wrote 10,000 words with a clock in every paragraph. Jason's October reset asks for the
opposite, and the reset outranks King. Here's how the register translates:

1. **Precision of place, not of the clock.** Opening on a clock time is retired outright
   (`voice/USED.md`), and reset rule 1 bans clock-time narration anywhere. King's exactness
   moves to rooms, objects and roads: the bleachers, the gas-station counter, the in-laws'
   driveway. A day of the week is fine. A minute is not.
2. **Every stamp is real.** The precision only works because it's true. The place comes from the
   export or the data, or it doesn't go in. The older files' advice to invent plausible details
   and mark them "I'm told" is void; the guide says never invent.
3. **One number a paragraph, as the receipt.** The numeral pile is out.
4. **Short sections that stand alone as texts.** Length was King's problem with NBC, and it's
   Jason's problem too. The bonus post and the memos are a few short texts, and the captions are
   a few lines. Only the season-in-review gets room, and even it goes out in sections that each
   work alone.
5. **Retired furniture** (`voice/USED.md`): numbered "Things I think" lists, "Factoid That May
   Interest Only Me," "Good for you, ___" as a fixed slot, "Now, to the rest of the week" and
   "Programming note." The `05-prompt-kit.md` checklist predates the reset. Its clock-time open,
   "Good for you," "never be short" and "each item starts *I think*" lines are void.

What still works, and where it goes:

- **The bonus-settled post and the Instagram award captions:** award-header deadpan, the
  attribution-line joke.
- **Bylaws, punishment and governance memos:** institutional earnestness, the gracious
  correction, the explicit pivot.
- **The Beer Mile:** embedded access, told by place and duration, never by clock.
- **The season-in-review:** the whole furniture set, self-grading, a worst-takes list for Wolf
  himself, an "[N]-for-[N]" memories feature from the 2021–2026 archive, a community award as
  the sincere beat, and a plug for Mike Hunt or Scott Van Pelvis (occasional, logged in
  `USED.md`).
- **Renames:** King's travel section puns on his own surname, so Wolf's is **On the Road**. The
  coffee and beer sub-items become **Last Call**: one beer, where he had it, one honest sentence.

The frames, vocabulary and traps, already adjusted to the reset, are in `07-phrasebook.md`.
