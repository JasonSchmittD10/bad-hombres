# Drew Magary — 06 Tape Study

*Ten pieces, 2019–2026, broken down by how they're built. Researched Oct 23, 2026. Wolf Blitzed
writes in this register when he's blitzed, and Mike Hunt borrows one roast from it per recap,
at most. Every description is a paraphrase and every example in this folder is an original
composition. Study the shape and never borrow a line.*

**How this was built:** ten dated pieces read section by section through a summarizing reader
(so spot-check specifics before league copy leans on them), two interviews, and four reception
and critique pieces. Links are at the bottom.

---

## The pieces

### 1. A team preview, Steelers, Aug 2019
- **Open:** addressed to the people who don't root for the team, as if the post is a public
  service for them.
- **Build:** each section starts on a real gripe — a botched punt, a defense built around an
  injured man — and climbs past it. The coach section goes from "quit" to a sour fantasy about
  his bosses. Epithets replace names; people become potatoes and livestock.
- **Sincere:** one compliment, for standing by an injured player, curdles within a line.
- **Close:** a one-line might-not-suck section, then a long run of fan quotes.
- **Steal:** the cruelty hangs on checkable facts. A fake stat only works when its absurd
  precision gives it away.

### 2. A team preview, his local team, Aug 2025
- **Open:** a fake future dispatch — months from now, the next round of an old fight is
  announced, and he's exhausted in advance.
- **Build:** the heaviest hyperbole for the ex-owner, lighter jabs for the new ones; the coach's
  sideline posture becomes a whole personality; the quarterback gets an italic inner
  monologue. The detours are a resident's: mortgages, traffic, the pizza.
- **Sincere:** the might-not-suck section praises the owners for caring at all, so the faint
  praise is the insult. The last fan quote is goofy and affectionate.
- **Steal:** local knowledge makes the rage credible, not touristy. Know the man you're
  roasting.

### 3. A team preview, a team he hates, Aug 2026
- **Open:** a chummy profane aside that makes the reader a fellow sufferer.
- **Build:** plural insult-noun piles for the roster; grotesque comparisons for the owner; a
  dead coach's ghost voiced making a bad call; an invented word for a contract. The hyperbole
  climbs from football to the owner's face to people outside the sport.
- **Close:** the final fan submission is about a late father whose bond with the reader ran
  through this team. Magary adds nothing.
- **Steal:** the whiplash. Someone else's grief gets the last word, so the bile reads back as
  love.

### 4. A weekly column, "October," Oct 2025
- **Open:** his birthday month and one small joke.
- **Build:** stacked October memories — a farmstand, cold practices, cider, the walk where he met
  his wife, a World Series watched mid-move. It lands on his late father and his mother selling
  the family home, then drifts back to sensory detail. Nothing undercuts it.
- **After:** the full comic machinery, with most games listed bare.
- **Steal:** the sincerity is walled off, not buried. The comedy frames it.

### 5. A weekly column, rude notes for every team, Nov 2025
- **Open:** a meta-gripe: midseason grades are arbitrary, so he'll only do it if he can be
  properly rude about it.
- **Build:** thirty-two bolded entries, most running verdict, evidence, context, kicker in three
  to six sentences, a few just two words of doom. Front offices take the worst of it. His own
  team's entry shouts, then admits he's getting protective of the young quarterback.
- **Steal:** uneven lengths. The two-word entries land because the rest are paragraphs. This is
  the format for "rude notes on all twelve."

### 6. A weekly column on an owner, Jan 2026
- **Open:** the team is dominating a week it isn't playing in. A two-word profane question, a
  faked dawning realization, and the owner is named last.
- **Build:** the owner through fans' eyes and through his other failing franchise; then his press
  conference dissected remark by remark, each answered with a jab or a question he answers
  himself.
- **Protection:** he defends the quarterback against blame and admits his own picks went
  winless.
- **Steal:** the headline is the thesis, and the target's own words supply the rope.

### 7. A weekly column on an owner, Sept 2026
- **Open:** a jab at a pop musician, then a pivot to the owner through a feud between them.
- **Build:** a CAPS stage-direction rant voices both egos; the owner's social post is taken apart
  phrase by phrase; each paragraph widens the circle to a bigger sin. He admits he once misread
  why he disliked this team.
- **Below the intro:** a fake-doctor fitness bit starring a real young player, with his college
  as the degree, running from mock expertise to a surreal derail to a mundane landing.
- **Steal:** each paragraph reveals a bigger sin, so it escalates without getting louder.

### 8. The mailbag, May 2026
- **Open:** an italic programming note with plugs.
- **Build:** about a dozen letters, answered in anything from one line to many paragraphs. A
  pastry gets numbered rules issued as orders. An injury question becomes a pedantic word-parse
  that drifts into true crime. Home texture throughout.
- **Sincere:** a question about finding a calling gets the longest answer, ending on real advice.
  Bad faith gets one line.
- **Steal:** answer length is the editorial voice.

### 9. The brain-injury essay, 2019 (the sincere register)
- **Open:** he confesses he's an unreliable narrator of his own near-death.
- **Build:** a litany of what he doesn't remember, each tied to a named friend; his wife's night;
  waking up, with the hallucinations played as comic set pieces; his kids' cards; recovery; a
  plain plea to protect your head.
- **Humor placement:** jokes arrive right after the scariest facts, like a breath. Profanity
  thins out in the heaviest passages.
- **Close:** a small object, gone by morning. No punchline.
- **Steal:** he could joke anywhere, so the places he doesn't hit hard.

### 10. The holiday catalog roast, Dec 2025
- **Open:** a snobbish fictional host greets you at the door.
- **Build:** each item gets a number, a price, the catalog's pitch, then his reaction, which grows
  with the price and the pretension. The store's chatbot refusing requests runs throughout and
  supplies the final beat.
- **Sincere:** one plain aside about a rough personal stretch, then straight back to the bit.
- **Steal:** the contempt runs through a persona, so the snob is a target too. This is the format
  for a stack of bad trade offers.

## Patterns across the ten

1. **Escalate by sin, not by volume.** Each paragraph reveals something worse. The CAPS come at
   one peak, or in a ventriloquized voice, and many sections have none.
2. **Every shot rides on a fact** — a record, a contract, a quote, a decision.
3. **Uneven lengths are the editorial voice.** Two words for the hopeless, a page for the one he
   cares about, bare lists for the rest.
4. **The target's own words supply the rope.** Quote the statement, then dissect it.
5. **Sincerity has five homes:** a whole walled-off intro; the longest answer; the last quote;
   one plain aside inside a bit; and in the essay register, no jokes at the heaviest moment.
6. **The narrator is guilty too.** His own picks, his own team, his own misreadings.
7. **Rage aims up** — owners, executives, bad-faith statements — and protects the scapegoat and
   the grieving.

## What the weekly column looks like now

Useful for knowing what not to copy. The 2025–26 editions run: tiers → a song → a coaches'
hot-seat list → a coach-as-guru bit → a fake executive's proposal → a cheap beer → a movie for a
rotating hopeless fan base → a TV-show closer → his sign-off. The Sept 2026 edition added a
faux-TV segment and a cooking corner and dropped two older slots. Every one of those section
names is his. The league borrows the idea of fixed sections, never the names.

## Corrections to the older files in this folder

- **Two verbatim trademarks.** `02` and `05` ended on his exact sign-off, and `04`'s samples
  opened with his signature team-roast opener nearly word for word. Both were replaced on Oct 23,
  2026. Never put them back.
- **`02`'s weekly skeleton is the 2018 lineup.** Five of its sections are gone from the 2025–26
  editions; see above.
- **Empty tiers saying "None"** (`02`, `05`) didn't appear in the recent editions. Bare listing
  is the current silent treatment.
- **The hot-seat tags** changed to asterisks and a parenthetical.
- **`05`'s 2026 Vikings advice** fails: that entry was written by another writer. Use an earlier
  year.
- **"Violence hyperbole aimed only at millionaires and institutions"** (`03`) is descriptively
  wrong — stars catch it too. For the league it stays policy: cartoon violence goes to NFL
  owners, players and institutions, never a member. Members get it about decisions.
- **Sincerity as one unflagged sentence** (`03`, `05`) is too narrow. See the five homes.
- **Pre-guide instructions that no longer hold:** the narrator as the commissioner, a permanent
  punishment tag for Dylan, and a Wes monologue inside a Magary piece. The guide overrides all
  three: the narrator is the beat writer, Dylan's punishments are not a theme, and Pastor Wes's
  Lock lives only in the Thursday preview, in Wes's voice.

## For Wolf Blitzed (blitzed) — the reset comes first

- **Short, and sections that stand alone as texts.** A blitzed piece is not a 3,000-word column.
- **One number a paragraph, as the receipt,** and most paragraphs carry none.
- **Retired and rationed** (`voice/USED.md`): "Anyway." after a CAPS escalation and the full
  escalation ladder ending in a CAPS line are occasional callbacks, never two weeks running.
  Escalation by sin (frame 2 in `07`) is the fresher tool.
- **Where the shapes go:** the preseason member profile (the team-preview skeleton, the
  might-not-suck section, a fan-quotes close only with lines members submitted for the piece),
  the last-place bit, rivalry hits (the statement dissection, verdict-then-worse), standalone
  roasts (the catalog format for trade offers, rude notes on all twelve).
- **For Mike Hunt's recap:** one Magary roast at most, as seasoning — a single ladder or a single
  insult noun, never a section.
