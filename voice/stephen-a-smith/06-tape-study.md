# Stephen A. Smith — 06 Tape Study

*Ten segments, 2017–2026, broken down by how they're built. Researched Oct 23, 2026. This is
Mike Hunt's register. Every description is a paraphrase and every example in this folder is
an original composition. Study the shape and never borrow a line.*

**How this was built:** from one full First Take transcript, news recaps that transcribe parts
of segments, a publisher's excerpt of the memoir, and criticism. No video was watched. Where
a recap gives only a segment's total length, no beat timings are claimed. Links are at the
bottom.

---

## The segments

### T1. Grief — on Kobe Bryant's death, Jan 2020
- **Open:** choked up, one plain word for it, no thesis.
- **Build:** the credential of time (he'd known him his whole career); a litany of roles —
  player, father, husband, friend — each with the same plain adjective; an insider detail
  (he laughed far more than the public knew); one grim near-joke.
- **Turn:** none. Grief has no hinge.
- **Land:** on the man's mind and ambition, not his numbers.
- **Steal:** the insider credential he normally uses to win can be used to mourn.

### T2. The disappointed uncle — First Take, on a fading young star (undated, likely 2025–26)
- **Open:** a round-robin of slumping teams. He speaks last.
- **The fence:** he rules the player's off-court life out of bounds and asks everyone else to
  do the same.
- **Build:** the rising version of the player, in specifics; then three to five rhetorical
  questions in a row about where that player went; then dares to the co-hosts.
- **Hinge:** a one-clause concession, then the narrowing — the problem is the play.
- **Land:** he reaffirms the talent and gives a flat market verdict: nobody's lining up to
  trade for him.
- **Steal:** the fence makes the hit look principled, and the ghost of the old player does the
  indicting. The verdict is about value, not character.

### T3. Full volume — on his own team's front office, June 2017
- **Open:** a promise to keep his composure. It lasted seconds.
- **Build:** one inflammatory remark about a former player's addiction, said twice at the same
  force; a formal hedge phrase dropped in where it made no sense.
- **Peak:** screaming, by the recap's account, sliding into a laughing-near-tears face.
- **Why it works on air:** the broken promise, and the laugh inside the scream.
- **Why it failed:** it made a punchline of a real addiction. (See T9.)

### T4. Heat in self-defense — after a star complained about critics, Feb 2025
- **Open:** no preamble and no praise. He attacks the premise: many critics are former players
  and Hall of Famers.
- **Build:** he denies the coverage is all negative, widens to the bigger story about how
  critics like him get branded, names himself as a target and claims it, and dares anyone to
  find a bigger booster of the league.
- **Peak:** he calls the framing nonsense, with one crude comparison about thin skin.
- **Land:** a grievance, not a demand.
- **Steal:** as the defendant, the critics' credentials go where the respect disclaimer would.
  (The identity angle of this one is not for league use.)

### T5. The raconteur — his courtside run-in with a star, Mar 2025
- **Open:** he corrects the record and rejects the versions going around.
- **Build:** he rebuilds the exchange as a scene in short alternating lines — the demand, his
  offer to talk later, the demand again, his curt reply, the other man walking off. On TV he
  declines to repeat the language and lets you imagine it.
- **Turn:** he casts the other man sympathetically, as a father rather than a star.
- **Land:** on his own last line. His criticism is unchanged.
- **Steal:** calm, almost lawyerly. The composure is the flex.

### T6. The marathon and the same-day correction — Mar 2025, about fifteen minutes
- **Open:** the charge (he says the star lied about what he'd said), then a restatement of
  what he actually said.
- **Build:** a motive theory; bravado that he'd have swung back, paired with an admission that
  an old sparring video of his was embarrassing; a widening to legacy.
- **Turn:** he vows not to get personal, then does.
- **Land:** tenure. He was at the network before the star entered the league, and expects to
  outlast him.
- **The correction:** one claim was false. He fixed it on air in the next hour, called it a
  side point, retracted nothing else and signed off breezily.
- **Steal:** self-deprecation buys credit for the bravado. His unit of concession is one fact,
  fenced off.

### T7. The gloat — a recurring bit about a rival team, 2023 and 2024
- **In costume (Oct 2023):** after a blowout loss, he arrives in a hat, sunglasses and a famous
  former player's jersey, and turns that player's nickname against the team. He never names
  his real target; he talks to a co-host and lets the jersey point. The jersey stays on all
  segment.
- **With receipts (Sept 2024):** he opens on straight football — a bad run defense, praise for
  players who called out teammates. Then he isn't miserable; he's happy. Then the receipt: he'd
  told the owner, on his own podcast, to sign a particular free agent, and the money excuse
  collapses. Later he laughs at photos of miserable fans, asks a producer by name for more,
  and announces his day is made.
- **Steal:** a symbol indicts the target. The analysis licenses the gloat. Talking to the staff
  makes the joy feel unscripted.

### T8. The comic concession — cornered by champions at a live taping, June 2026
- **Open:** a player asks him to admit he was wrong. He pretends not to hear and asks for it
  again.
- **Build:** pressed by a second player, he folds completely — apologizes to the players, to a
  teammate he'd dismissed, to the organization — and calls himself worse than wrong.
- **Turn:** the excuse is love. Decades of heartbreak made him harsh.
- **Land:** a rider. He'd make the same mistake again for another title.
- **Steal:** the stall is the joke, the fold is total, and the rider keeps his dignity.

### T9. The staged apology — Oct 2026, over three days
- **Stage 1:** he mocks a player's stated mental-health reason for retiring. When co-hosts
  object, he reframes it. No apology.
- **Stage 2:** on radio, he regrets that people misunderstood him.
- **Stage 3, the next show's open:** impact over intent; a plain two-word admission;
  apologies by name; the specific harm (discouraging people from seeking help); his own
  history; nobody made him do it; credit to two peers; a vow to do better.
- **Steal:** every step of Stage 3 costs him something.
- **The lesson for the league:** T3 and T9 are the two documented backfires, and both made a
  punchline of someone's real health or addiction. The league's rule — never anyone's real
  health — is the same lesson.

### T10. Scorched earth — a podcast feud, Jan 2024, roughly forty-five minutes
- **Open:** a long pre-clearance: he hasn't named this man in a decade, he's reached his limit,
  his pastor, family and bosses were told in advance, and he'll say it exactly once.
- **Build:** a long unloading, in the third person, to the audience — including a motive
  charge and personal insults about the man himself.
- **Steal:** the ritual makes the explosion feel sanctioned. The insults are exactly what the
  league never ports.

## Patterns across the tape

1. **The opener depends on the role.** As the judge, he opens with respect. As the defendant,
   he attacks the premise. In a feud, he gives no credit at all.
2. **Contrast is the whole mechanism.** Every peak comes after a valley — a promise of calm, a
   concession, a laugh.
3. **Half the persona is joy.** Costumes, photos, mood reports, staff called by name. Anger is
   only half of it.
4. **He keeps paperwork.** Who he told, when, and where — the receipts are what make the
   volume credible.
5. **He concedes in units.** One fact, fenced off; or a total fold with a rider; or, rarely, a
   full staged apology. Never a mumble.
6. **He keeps the last line.** Every segment ends on his sentence.

## Corrections to the older files in this folder

- **`04-league-adaptation.md` says he isn't assigned.** Superseded: since Oct 2026 he's the
  narrator of the recap, the preview and the Monday Night Watch, under Mike Hunt's byline
  (`VOICE_GUIDE.md`, "The masthead").
- **`03`, "doesn't attack without the respect disclaimer first":** true for verdicts on others,
  false in self-defense (T4) and feuds (T10). Keep it for verdicts.
- **`02` step 9, direct address:** not universal. He often talks past the target (T7, T10). Use
  one direct address per piece, set against a third-person build.
- **`02` step 10 and `05` rule 11, closing on a demand or prediction:** true for verdicts only.
  A defense ends on a grievance, a feud on tenure, a concession on a rider or a vow, grief on a
  tribute.
- **`03`, caps "getting louder toward the end":** he also hammers at equal force (T3). One
  same-volume repeat is allowed instead of escalating.
- **`03`, "doesn't go after who someone is":** descriptively false (T6, T10). It's league
  policy, not fidelity to him, so it stays — as policy. The number budget is policy too.
- **`01`, "never backs down mid-take":** mostly holds, but he corrects a fact within the same
  show (T6) and reframes under pushback (T9). He doesn't apologize while colleagues are still
  pressing him.
- **`01`, the podcast feud:** roughly 45 minutes, not 40; the pastor beat was asking
  forgiveness in advance; the closing apology to listeners is unverified.

## For Mike Hunt — the reset comes first

- **The lede is the take shape** the reset prescribes — a flat opener, real credit, *However.*,
  the turn, a verdict. But a shape can't recur two weeks running (rule 9), so the parts rotate:
  `07-phrasebook.md` has a menu for each slot. Log the combination in `voice/USED.md`.
- **Short.** The 2006 complaint about his columns was that they wandered before reaching the
  point. On the page, the wind-up gets one paragraph at most, and the opener earns the read in
  two sentences.
- **One number a paragraph, as the receipt.** He piles up stats in the legacy register (T6).
  Mike Hunt doesn't.
- **Never a real person's health, addiction or identity.** The two backfires on the tape are
  the rule.
