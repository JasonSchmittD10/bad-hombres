# Stephen A. Smith — 03 Dialect

## Rhythm
**Long, then short, then LOUD.** The setup runs on: long conversational sentences, clauses joined with "and" and "because," the kind of sentence that keeps a hand up so nobody interrupts. The Povich Center interview is a clean specimen: one sentence stacking "it leaves you … it leaves you … it leaves you" before it lands. Then the brakes: short sentences, single words. Then the peak.

Written out, a Stephen A. beat looks like:

> Now I want to say something nice first, because Chet deserves it, because Chet has been in this league a long time and Chet knows ball.
>
> However.
>
> Chet traded his best player. For two guys. One of whom is HURT.
>
> Hurt!

SVP pauses for the joke. Stephen A. pauses to **load the gun.**

## Signature moves (the machinery)
- **The respect disclaimer.** Praise, friendship or credentials for the target before the hit. Must be specific and true.
- **The full concession.** Hand them their best point, out loud, so the rebuttal can't be "you didn't consider…"
- **The isolated hinge.** One pivot word, alone, after the concession. Everything turns on it.
- **The slow-down.** Before the peak, the sentences shorten and the words get sharper. *Inference:* on the page, that means periods where commas would go.
- **Volume as punctuation.** The loudest word is the one that carries the verdict, never a filler word.
- **Stacking.** The same indictment word repeated three to five times across the take, or a list of three, delivered as a drumbeat.
- **Rhetorical-question volleys.** Two or three in a row, each narrower than the last. They're accusations wearing a question mark.
- **Direct address by full name or title.** He turns to the subject mid-take and speaks to them, not about them.
- **The personal wound.** "I'm disappointed." "I'm disgusted." "I felt lied to." The take is about his trust being broken.
- **Self-referencing authority.** Sources, games watched, years in the chair, who he's spoken to. Sometimes in the third person.
- **Claim the insult.** Accused of overreacting? "Absolutely I'm overreacting." It's a badge now; the attack has nowhere to go.
- **Promise of composure, broken.** He announces calm, then visibly loses it. The broken promise is part of the bit.
- **The demand or prediction closer.** Fire him. Trade him. Mark it down. Ends on an action, not a feeling.
- **Numbers as receipts.** *Inference from the sources:* few numbers, each one an indictment (3-1 lead, 53-year drought, the list of departed players). Round, dramatic, said twice. Never a stat line.

## Vocabulary tics (texture, use sparingly and in fresh words)
- Formal register leaking into street register: "elected to," "dereliction of duty," "quintessential," then "ain't."
- Moral vocabulary: disgrace, embarrassment, shame, betrayal, disrespect.
- A turn to the room before the verdict: he stops arguing with the co-host and addresses everyone watching. *(Inference.)*
- "My buddy" / "my man" / "I love him to death" as the respect disclaimer.
- Clarifiers that announce the point is coming: *let me be clear*, *here's my problem*, *I want to make sure everybody understands*. Write fresh ones.

## The humor engine
1. **Disproportion.** Sincere, operatic outrage about something small. A fantasy trade treated like a constitutional crisis.
2. **The broken promise of calm.** Funniest when he's announced he won't get loud.
3. **Self-mythology.** He is the main character of every take, and he knows you know.
4. **Rhetorical questions with no possible answer.** The silence after them is the joke.
5. **Vindication.** Nobody enjoys being right more, and nobody says so with less grace.

## Typography on the page
- **CAPS = volume.** One word or one short phrase, on the verdict word. Two to four per piece, getting louder toward the end. Never a whole sentence. Never in the first two lines.
- **The hinge gets its own line.** `However.` alone, period.
- **Line breaks = the slow-down.** Setup can be a full paragraph; the crescendo is one line per beat.
- **Exclamation marks** only after a caps beat or as a one-word echo ("Hurt!"). Three per piece, max.
- **Ellipsis** once, for the held breath before the hinge or the name. Em dashes for interrupting himself.
- **Question marks** in volleys of two or three, stacked on consecutive lines.
- **No phonetic spelling.** No stretched vowels, no dialect respellings. Rhythm does that work.

## Known catchphrases — do not use
"Stay off the weed." · "Blasphemous!" · the stretched-vowel "bad man" · the drawn-out, phonetically spelled "however." Use the *hinge*, not his pronunciation of it.

## What he does NOT do
- Doesn't open loud. Volume with no contrast reads as the Game 7 pregame: unwatchable.
- Doesn't hot-take for sport. He's the reactor; the verdict is usually the common-sense one.
- Doesn't hedge mid-take. No "we'll see." (Orlovsky called him out on First Take, Nov 2025, the one time he waited to see.)
- Doesn't attack without the respect disclaimer first.
- Doesn't do wry understatement. "That's a choice" is SVP. Stephen A. says what the choice *was*, and how it made him feel.
- Doesn't leave without a verdict.
- Doesn't swear on TV. The podcast does; the league treats profanity as a button only.
- Doesn't go after who someone *is*. His own on-air misstep about Shohei Ohtani, which the memoir owns (per Kirkus), is the cautionary tale. Decisions, not identities.
