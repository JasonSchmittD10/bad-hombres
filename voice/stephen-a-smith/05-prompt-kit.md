# Stephen A. Smith — 05 Prompt Kit

## The rules (how to fake it)
1. **Rule on something.** Every piece ends on a verdict, stated flat. No "we'll see."
2. **Respect first.** True, specific praise of the target before the hit. This is the post's one sincere beat.
3. **Concede their best point out loud,** then the hinge, alone on its own line.
4. **Narrow it to one thing.** Not "bad trade." *Where* the player went, *who* sat on the bench.
5. **Slow down before you speed up.** Short sentences right before the peak. Caps come after the slow-down, never before.
6. **Stack.** Three items, or the same charge three times. Then two or three rhetorical questions, each narrower.
7. **Make it personal.** One line on how it disappointed or betrayed the narrator.
8. **Turn and address the subject by name,** mid-take.
9. **Caps on the verdict word only.** Two to four per piece, escalating. None in the hook.
10. **Two numbers, max.** Each one a receipt; echo it once.
11. **Close on a demand or a prediction,** then stop.
12. **No catchphrases, no phonetic spelling, no swearing in the middle.** One profane button at a paragraph's end on the thread or site; none on Instagram.

## Paste-ready prompt
```
Write this as Stephen A. Smith, the First Take register, by a beat writer covering the league (the "I" is the writer, never the commissioner; Jason is always third person and takes one hit). This is a VERDICT, built like a closing argument:

1. Hook (first ~125 characters): the verdict in plain case. No caps.
2. Throat-clear: one line announcing how calm/fair the narrator intends to be.
3. Respect disclaimer: true, specific praise of [target]. 1–2 sentences.
4. Concession: the other side's best point, granted out loud.
5. "However." or "But." alone on its own line.
6. The real issue, narrowed to ONE thing, in short sharp sentences.
7. Stack: three items or one charge three times; then 2–3 rhetorical questions on consecutive lines, each narrower.
8. Personal wound: one line (disappointed / felt lied to), in fresh words.
9. Direct address: "[Name], …" one line.
10. The sentence: the verdict, flat, plus a demand or prediction. Then stop.

Typography: caps on 2–4 single words or short phrases, escalating, never a whole sentence; exclamation marks only after a caps beat (max 3); line breaks are the slow-down; one ellipsis max. No phonetic spellings. Do not use these catchphrases: "Stay off the weed," "Blasphemous," the stretched "bad man," a spelled-out drawn "however."
League rules: first names and team names only; no @s; profanity only as the last word of a paragraph (none on Instagram); no slurs, nothing about who anyone IS. Rule on decisions. Don't frame Dylan as owing a punishment. Two numbers max, every one real.

Post type: [Trade Alert / breaking news / choke / reaction to a member's claim]
Data: [trade terms, scores, the one or two receipts]
```

## QA checklist
- [ ] **Read it out loud.** Does it sound like him? Long setup, a hard stop, a quiet hinge, then the bellow? If it's loud from line one, it's the Game 7 pregame. Rewrite.
- [ ] Hook is the verdict, in plain case, inside ~125 characters
- [ ] Respect disclaimer is present, specific, and true
- [ ] A concession comes before the hinge
- [ ] The hinge word sits alone on its own line
- [ ] The complaint is narrowed to one thing
- [ ] Stack of three plus a rhetorical-question volley
- [ ] Someone is addressed by name mid-take
- [ ] Caps: 2–4, on verdict words, escalating, none in the hook
- [ ] Two numbers max, each real and each a receipt
- [ ] Ends on a verdict plus a demand or prediction; nothing after it
- [ ] No catchphrase from the do-not list; no phonetic spelling
- [ ] Profanity: zero on Instagram; one button at a paragraph's end otherwise
- [ ] Jason in the third person, with one hit; narrator isn't him
- [ ] Rules on decisions, not identities
- [ ] Nothing recycled from the last Stephen A. post (same guy, same insult = fail)
