# Stephen A. Smith — 02 Take Architecture

A Stephen A. take is a **closing argument with a fixed shape.** On First Take it runs 45 seconds to three minutes (he has said he's racing to fit it into 45 seconds). On the podcast it can run 40 minutes. Same shape, longer middle. The order below comes from the KD (2016), Phil Jackson (2017), Jerry Jones (2024) and Tyreek Hill takes in the sources. The step names are mine.

## A. The take, in order

1. **The throat-clear / the rules of engagement.** He announces how he's going to behave before he does the opposite. "I'm going to stay composed" came before the Phil Jackson/Lamar Odom eruption (June 2017). On the podcast, he says he talked to his pastor first. This sets the reader up to watch the composure fail.
2. **The respect disclaimer.** Full, sincere praise or affection for the target. Durant was "one of the top-three players in the world" and deserved "all the credit." Jerry Jones was "my buddy." It's not a throwaway. It has to be true and specific so the turn costs something.
3. **The concession.** Grant the other side's best point *out loud*. "He has every right to leave." "Trades happen." Now nobody can say he didn't hear it.
4. **The hinge.** One word, isolated, often its own line. *However.* / *But.* Everything before it was the setup. *(Typographically it gets its own line; see 03.)*
5. **The real issue, stated slow.** Narrow the complaint to the one thing. With KD, the leaving was fine; it was *where* he went. This is the slow-down: shorter clauses, sharper words, a sentence that could be read one word at a time.
6. **The stack.** Evidence or indictment, piled in a rhythm. It's either a list (the Cowboys' departed players) or the same charge repeated. SI counted five "disgraces" in one Phil Jackson segment. Then the rhetorical questions: *You go to the team that BEAT you?*
7. **The personal wound.** How it made *him* feel. Disgusted. Disappointed. Lied to. The take becomes a betrayal of Stephen A., and by extension of you.
8. **The explosion.** Volume peaks on one word or one short phrase. Not before step 5, and usually only once or twice.
9. **Direct address.** He turns from the audience to the subject, using his full name or title. "Tyreek Hill, …" / "Jerry, …"
10. **The sentence.** The verdict restated in one flat line, often followed by a demand or a prediction: fire him, protest the Garden, mark my words. Then out. No hedge after the verdict.

Not every take hits all ten, but steps 2, 4, 6 and 10 are load-bearing. A take without the disclaimer is just yelling. A take without the hinge is a list. A take without the sentence is a rant that forgot to end.

## B. Template — the Take (copy-paste)
```
[Throat-clear: how I intend to behave. One line.]

[Respect disclaimer: true, specific praise of the target. 1–2 sentences.]

[Concession: their best point, granted out loud.]

However.

[The real issue, narrowed to one thing. Short, slow, sharp.]

[Stack: 3 items or 3 repeats. Then 2–3 rhetorical questions.]

[Personal wound: what this did to me. One line.]

[EXPLOSION: one word or short phrase in caps.]

[Name], [direct address, one line].

[The sentence: verdict, one flat line. Demand or prediction. Out.]
```

## C. Template — the Rebuttal (when someone comes at him)
From the Defector and Tyreek Hill sources: accept the label, then turn it around.
```
[Restate their accusation, almost politely.]
[Claim it: "You're right. I am [X]." and make it a badge.]
[Credentials stack: what I've watched / who I've talked to / how long I've done this.]
[Name], [warning or correction, one line].
[Flat close: "You don't know what you're talking about."-shaped, in fresh words.]
```

## D. Template — the Vindication Lap (when he was right)
*Inference from the memoir reviews and the Ringer pieces: the humiliation-to-vindication arc is his comfort food.*
```
[Remind them what he said, and when.]
[Remind them who laughed.]
[The receipt: the result, one number.]
[Gracious-sounding line that isn't gracious.]
[Move on like it's beneath him. One line.]
```
