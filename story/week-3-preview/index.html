Week 3 · The Preview

Seventh Against Eighth, and God Help Us, It Matters

Chris and Wes were a coin flip on Tuesday, Hoa has four empty chairs in his lineup, and the rest of Week 3 is rated in Money Shots.

From the BoothThe Bad Hombres beat · Week 3 · Sep 24, 2026

First, the commissioner. Jason Schmitt is 2-0 and carries the highest projection in the league into Week 3, and he is doing it with Tyler Shough at quarterback, a man who was on his bench last Monday night and has since been promoted from emergency contact to the plan.

Now the week's one story. On Tuesday, Yahoo could not tell Chris and Wes apart: two 1-1 teams, seventh and eighth, parked one and two spots under the playoff line. That's a coin flip, and in Week 3 a coin flip should mean nothing. It will not mean nothing. Whoever wins walks out 2-1 and gets to spend Monday refreshing the standings page with his thumb, and whoever loses is 1-2 and gets to spend Monday explaining what "a long season" means to men who did not ask. Picture Wes on Tuesday morning, standing in front of the church bulletin board, pinning up the standings next to the potluck sign-up. SIXTH PLACE, BROTHERS. SIXTH PLACE IS A GIFT, AND THE GIFT IS GIVEN TO THEM THAT START SAQUON.

Anyway.

The Games

All six, rated for sheer watchability on a scale of one to five Money Shots.

FIVE MONEY SHOTS

Chris vs. Wes. The Game of the Week. It was 0.36 on Tuesday, and since then Chris's side of the ledger has quietly gotten heavier while Wes's has not moved, which is how every close game in this league starts: a small gap, a long weekend, and one man pretending he hasn't noticed. Wes will roll out Dak Prescott, Justin Jefferson and a Saquon Barkley with a Q next to his name, a lineup you'd have been proud to draft off a magazine rack. Chris will roll out a rookie running back named Jeremiyah Love and a tight end, Brock Bowers, who is also questionable, so nobody here is sleeping well Saturday. Chris leads the series 3-2. That's the only thing anybody can say for sure.

FOUR MONEY SHOTS

None.

THREE MONEY SHOTS

Jason vs. David. The series is 4-4, and the two-time defending champion is 0-2 and starting Parker Washington in his flex, with Stefon Diggs and Chris Godwin, two men who have been to Pro Bowls, sitting on his bench in their good clothes. David has won this league twice by knowing things the rest of us don't. Let us assume he knows something about Parker Washington. Let us assume that very hard.

Dylan vs. Adam. Dylan's reward for last week is the man in first place. Dylan leads their series 4-2, which is not the sentence you expected to read in this spot, and is also true.

Matt vs. Zack. Of all the managers in this league, Matt is the one Zack has figured out: four wins in five tries. Zack is 0-2 and has done very little wrong to get there. If there is a week for the schedule to finally hand him one, this is the one I'd pick.

TWO MONEY SHOTS

Tola vs. Drew. Drew is 0-2, at the bottom, and starting Puka Nacua and Travis Etienne, both questionable, with Justin Herbert on his bench, which means Sunday morning Drew will be standing in his kitchen reading the inactive list like a man in the hallway at 3 a.m. trying to work out which smoke detector is chirping. Tola is starting George Kittle at flex. Tola is fine.

ONE MONEY SHOT

Hoa vs. Erick. As of Thursday afternoon, Hoa has no quarterback in his lineup. He also has no second running back, no second receiver and no flex. Four empty chairs. Joe Burrow, Kyler Murray and D'Andre Swift are on his bench, healthy, waiting for someone to come get them, and Yahoo has projected the whole operation for 64.47. This is Pastor Wes's Lock. Erick, meanwhile, is starting Dallas Goedert, who is listed doubtful, in the week the $9 goes to the lowest-scoring tight end, which means Erick may win the bonus by owning a man who stays home. Hoa. Please. Set the lineup.

Pastor Wes’s Lock of the Week

Brothers, turn with me to the Letter to the Kickers, chapter two. “And the lot fell upon the one nobody wanted, and the lot was honest.”

Now Tuesday I was out front changing the letters on the church sign. We had our message picked out, eight words, and I get up on the stepladder and I am two E’s short. Two. So there I am in the heat with a bag of consonants trying to say something true about grace using the letters I have got, and I want to tell you, you can say a great deal with what is left over. You cannot say everything. I put up FEED THE FLOCK and I have not remembered since what the rest of it was going to be. It was good, though. It was going to be good.

And standing in that parking lot holding two K’s I did not need, it came to me: the man with four empty chairs in his lineup is still a man with a lineup. Hoa over Erick. Amen.

(Wes: 0–1.)

Lineups lock at kickoff. Set them.
