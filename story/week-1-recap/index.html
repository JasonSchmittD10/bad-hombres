The Competition’s Level Was 150.64

Zack scores 146.16 and loses to the one man he makes look good, Jason posts the high score with 35.80 on his bench, Dylan sets the floor, and Matt takes the Money Shot by 0.40.

It was 8:42 Thursday morning when Jason asked the league thread how Zack had become the only man Adam holds a winning record against.

Two minutes later Zack answered. “i play to my competitions level,” he wrote. By Monday night he had scored 146.16, the fourth-best total in the league, and lost to Adam by 4.48.

The question was fair. In five seasons Adam has gone 28-42, and the only line on his head-to-head card with more wins than losses reads Zack, 4-1.

“While also having the most wins against david, i play to my competitions level” — Zack, 8:44 a.m. Thursday

The first half checks out, and he deserves that on the record. Zack is 5-1 against David, the two-time defending champion. Nobody else in the league has more than four wins against David.

The second half is where it went sideways. Adam, whose 112.95 career scoring average is the lowest of anyone in the league’s five seasons, scored 150.64 in Week 1. That is 37.69 points above his own average and the third-best score of the week. Zack played to the competition’s level. The competition’s level had moved.

Zack’s 146.16 would have beaten eight of the other eleven teams in the league. Caleb Williams put up 38.26 of it, which brings us to the second part of this story.

The other 0.40.

The Week 1 bonus, Josh Allen’s Money Shot, goes to the highest-scoring starting quarterback. Caleb Williams finished with 38.26. Josh Allen finished with 38.66. The $9 went to Matt.

So in one week Zack lost his matchup by 4.48 to a manager who holds a winning record against nobody else, and lost the bonus by 0.40 to the quarterback the award is named after. The series with Adam is 5-1.

Now, to the rest of the week.

The awards

Big Dick of the Week: Jason, 154.50. Yahoo projected Pull-Out Game Weak for 117.92. It beat that by 36.58, the biggest overshoot in the league, and won its matchup by 26.54, the widest margin of the week. That is 24.57 above his career average. Credit where it is owed, and it is owed.

Little Bitch of the Week: Dylan, 101.86. The only score in the league under 113. It is 11.78 below Dylan’s own career average, and that average already belongs to the man with the worst winning percentage in league history, .329. He did not need the extra help. He found it anyway, mostly in the kicker slot.

Josh Allen’s Money Shot — $9 to Matt. Josh Allen, 38.66. Second was Zack’s Caleb Williams at 38.26; third was Tola’s Lamar Jackson at 28.96. The lowest-scoring starting quarterback in the league, David’s Bo Nix, finished 33.22 behind the winner, which is a number we will come back to shortly. The award is named after Matt’s quarterback, and Matt’s quarterback cashed it.

The matchups

Wes 127.30, David 122.54. David has won the last two championships in this league. In Week 1 he started Bo Nix, who scored 5.44, the fewest of any starting quarterback, while Patrick Mahomes sat on his bench and scored 22.66. Put Mahomes in and David scores 139.76 and wins by 12.46. He did not put Mahomes in. He parked the Ferrari under a tarp and drove the lawn tractor to his title defense, and the lawn tractor lost to the man who finished last in 2025. David still leads the series, 5-3. Anyway.

Hoa 113.36, Dylan 101.86. Hoa won with the eleventh-best score of the week, which is like winning a hot-dog contest by being the second man to throw up. Ja’Marr Chase gave him 3.20. On the other side, Dylan’s kicker, Brandon Aubrey, scored 0.00. His defense, the Titans, scored 1.50. Jaylen Waddle, in the flex, scored 1.20. Three lineup spots, 2.70 points. Jordan Mason scored 11.90 on the bench, and here is the part I can’t stop thinking about: put Mason in for Waddle and Dylan still loses, by 0.80. The lineup wasn’t the problem. Kenneth Walker scored 36.10, 35 percent of the team, and dragged the rest of that roster around the field like a groomsman hauling the best man to the car at 1 a.m.

Jason 154.50, Drew 127.96. Drew opened the season as this column’s No. 1 power-ranked team. His kicker, Harrison Mevis, scored 1.00. His defense, the Texans, scored −1.00. Two roster spots, a combined zero, a perfect little donut. Jordan Love scored 23.48 on the bench while Justin Herbert started and scored 13.26; make that switch and Drew still loses by 16.32. The preseason No. 1 is ninth in the standings. The series, which Drew led 4-3, is tied 4-4.

Matt 132.66, Chris 116.42. Josh Allen and Derrick Henry scored 73.96 between them, more than half of Matt’s total. The other seven starters scored 58.70, including Terry McLaurin at 3.40 and a Chargers defense at 1.50, which is a lot of guys riding in the back of a two-man pickup. Chris started Tony Pollard, who scored 4.40, while Deebo Samuel scored 18.00 on his bench. Drake Maye gave him 9.82, eleventh of twelve starting quarterbacks. Matt leads the series 4-3.

Erick 153.52, Tola 134.46. Tola’s 134.46 was the fifth-best score of the week. It beat seven teams. It did not beat the one Tola was playing, because Erick got 30.00 from Zay Flowers, 28.90 from David Montgomery, 27.72 from Jalen Hurts and 25.70 from Dallas Goedert — four starters over 25 on their own. Erick beat his projection by 32.31. Tola got 13.80 from Christian McCaffrey and 5.50 from Drake London, who played like a man who came along to hold the jockstraps. Erick leads the series 4-3.

Adam 150.64, Zack 146.16. Sewp, I need you to hear this.

Yahoo had you by 3.32. You finished at 146.16. That score beats eight teams in this league. On almost any other schedule, you’ve won. Sewp, you’ve WON.

Then Christian Watson scores 34.70 for Adam.

Then Amon-Ra St. Brown scores 28.70, and DJ Moore scores 23.00.

Adam’s tight end, Kyle Pitts, scores 0.00. His kicker scores 2.00. It doesn’t matter.

And on your bench the whole time: Emmett Johnson, 8.80. In your flex: Courtland Sutton, 3.10.

Swap them and you win by 1.22.

You lost by 4.48.

Never set a lineup. Ever.

Zack’s Luck Index: −4. Third-most points against in the league, seventh in the standings. It’s the first reading of the season, so there is no movement to report. It simply starts in the red.

Things I think

1. I think the Ride or Die looks right after one week, and I think it looks right for reasons I didn’t give. The draft column sold Erick on Jalen Hurts in the sixth round and a backfield built three deep. Hurts delivered 27.72. Josh Jacobs, one of the three, scored 0.00 on the bench. The real engine was Zay Flowers and Dallas Goedert, 55.70 between them, and I mentioned neither.

2. I think this column owes D’Andre Swift an apology. The draft piece called him a strange choice as Hoa’s lead back. Swift scored 32.40, the most on Hoa’s roster, while Ja’Marr Chase scored 3.20. (a) Hoa also left Kyle Monangai’s 22.40 on the bench. (b) He won anyway, which is the most Hoa sentence available.

3. I think the commissioner should look at his own bench before he looks at anyone else’s. Jalen Coker scored 35.80 for Jason — from the bench. It was the highest score by any bench player in the league, and only three starters anywhere beat it: Josh Allen, Caleb Williams and Kenneth Walker. (a) Jason started A.J. Brown (5.60) and Luther Burden III (9.50) instead. (b) He still posted the week’s high score, which will make this lesson impossible to teach him.

4. I think two men started a tight end who scored 0.00 — Wes with Colston Loveland, Adam with Kyle Pitts — and I think both of them won, and I think everyone whose tight end actually scored should be allowed to file a grievance.

5. I think the standings are telling one small lie. Hoa is sixth, in a playoff spot, with the eleventh-best score in the league. Tola is eighth with the fifth-best. Zack is seventh with the fourth-best. My power rankings have Zack fourth and Hoa ninth, and I would do it again.

6. I think this is the part where I say something nice. (a) Adam has the lowest career scoring average of anyone in the league’s five seasons, 112.95 over 70 games, and he has heard about it for all 70. (b) He scored 150.64 in Week 1, beat his projection by 27.55, and is third in the standings. Good for you, Adam.

Looking ahead

Game of the Week: Erick vs. Zack. It leads because it’s a coin flip. Yahoo has Zack at 117.56 and Erick at 116.42, 1.14 apart, and the all-time series is tied 4-4. Last week these two posted the second- and fourth-best scores in the league, and only one of them has a win to show for it. I think Zack wins, by less than ten.

Jason vs. Wes. First place against fifth, projected 113.51 to 112.56, a 0.95 gap. Jason leads the series 5-2. I think Wes wins, and the commissioner’s stay in first place lasts exactly one week.

David vs. Dylan. The two-time defending champion is 0-1 and a 4.73-point underdog — to this week’s Little Bitch. Yahoo has Dylan at 116.45 and David at 111.72, and the series is 3-3. I think David wins by more than 15.

Pastor Wes’s Lock of the Week.

Brothers, if you’ll turn with me to the Epistle to the Waiver Wire, chapter nine, verse zero. “Blessed is he who setteth his lineup on the Saturday, for on the Sunday he shall rest.” Now I went to the hardware store this weekend for one hinge. One hinge. And there’s a man in aisle nine who wants to tell me about his gutters, and I’m standing there holding the hinge, and it came to me that this is what the Word means about patience, and also that I did not know what size hinge I needed. I still don’t. The hinge is in the truck.

But somewhere between the fasteners and the paint counter the Spirit moved, and it said: Hoa over Adam. Amen.

(Wes: 0–0.)
